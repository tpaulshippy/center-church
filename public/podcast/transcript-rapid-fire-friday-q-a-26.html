For it is impossible in the case of those who have once been enlightened, who have tasted the heavenly gift and have shared in the Holy Spirit and have tasted the goodness of the word of God and the powers of the age to come, And then have fallen away, to restore them again to repentance, since they are crucifying once again the Son of God to their own harm and holding him up to contempt.

Rich Richardson: Amy, that's a great question. There's a lot of debate. There's a few warning passages in Hebrews that are pretty strident, and this is one of them.

Now, one of the things we need to read this in is in light of Hebrews 3:14.

It says, for we have come to share in Christ, if indeed we hold our original confidence firm to the end. And so, one of the things that marks out a true believer is someone who holds on to Christ all the way to the end. Now, there are many people who seem to be holding on to Christ right now, but if they fall away, that's evidence that they're not genuine believers. So, and this is really difficult to understand because sometimes it seems like, hey, there are people who trust Jesus, who live for Jesus, but then when hardships come, they fall away and they stop following Jesus, right? And so that That's kind of— that's what the writer to the Hebrews is talking about in chapter 6, right? Sometimes you see people who seem to have been enlightened, who seem to have tasted the heavenly gift, but turn away.

And so, those people are not— if they turn away and fall away, it's proof that they were never Christians. And so, the idea here is there is no restoration for somebody who hasn't actually first been saved in the first place, right?

Rich Richardson: Yeah, right, exactly. And so, let me just put it in a different way. You know, like, I've been— having been a pastor for a number of years, there's a lot of people who I would have thought, hey, not a lot, but a handful that I would have thought, this person's a Christian, they believe the right things about God, they seem to be following the Lord, but then a certain hardship will rise up and they'll turn away and stop following Jesus. And so, what we think there is that this person has not held their confidence in Christ all the way to the end, as the writer of Hebrews says in chapter 3, verse 14. And so, in chapter 6, verses 4 through 6, this is another example where we're told that essentially, if you don't keep following Jesus, you should have no confidence that you're a Christian. And so, That's that's the idea, and so I mean some of the some of the debate gets to you know what does it mean to have been enlightened?

What does it mean to have tasted the heavenly gift? What does it mean to have shared in the Holy Spirit? Now I would say what that means is you've got people who are in the church who taste and see and experience at some level these things, maybe not personally but corporately, and yet they can still fall away if they haven't internalized. Their need for a Savior and repented on their own for their own sins and continue to follow Jesus to the end. So one of the signs to follow Jesus is not the miracles you do, the sermons you preach. I mean, Matthew chapter 7 says there's gonna be a lot of people who say, I did all this stuff in your name. Jesus isn't gonna say, no, you didn't. He's gonna say, you didn't know me. And so miracles and signs and wonders, those are not proof that we're Christians.

Proof is that we stay faithful to Jesus all the way to the end, that we fight sin all the way to the end, that we fight to obey and follow him all the way to the end, and that we don't walk away from him and stop serving him. And so, that's the warning here in Hebrews chapter 6.

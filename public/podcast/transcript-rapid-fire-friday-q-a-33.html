Rich Richardson: That's a whole cluster of questions for sure. Ready? Go. Yeah.

So To be reformed is, so I don't use the word Calvinist because Calvin Calvinism has a bad connotation, and so whatever if someone ever comes up to me and says, "Are you Calvinist?" I'll say, "What do you mean by that?" And usually they'll have a misunderstanding of double predestination. I do not believe in double predestination the way most people understand it. And so, but if you read Calvin, actually, if you read his institutes, you realize, man, this guy was a gifted theologian. And so to say we're Reformed means that we're a part of the Reformed tradition that broke away from the Catholic Church in the 1500s to try to reform the Catholics. That's why it's Reformed, right? And our belief is that God is sovereign, meaning he's in control, and he's good, meaning that he can only always do good.

And yet he's in control of all things.

And so even salvation. An Arminian, Not an Arminian, but an Arminian believes that a person has genuine free will and they have the free will to decide, and that there's no such thing as predestination in the way that a Reformed person understands it. So a Reformed person is going to say God chose without seeing anything good or bad in a person, only bad. An Arminian is going to say God looked through the corridors of time. And saw who would choose him and say, "Okay, I choose you." And so that's what an Armenian believes. I don't think that's what the word predestination means. It doesn't. But at the same time, Armenian folks are—it's not as if they're heresy. That's not a heresy. That's—it's—it's a—it's what I would say is a misunderstanding of how to understand the Bible.

But that doesn't mean they're outside of Christ. So reformed is a general synonym for Calvinist. But not quite. And not Reformed is a general synonym for Arminian. So charismatic, I don't like that word either because that word indicates things on TV that is not— Charismania? Yeah, it's not in line with the scripture. So I don't use the word charismatic at all anymore. I think we lost the word because of all kinds of subjective experiences that people have out there on TV that's just not helpful. I say the word continuationist. And so a cessationist, so really it's a cessationist is somebody who believes that the miraculous gifts have ceased.

They were only available for the first century church. And when the apostles died out, the miraculous gifts ceased. And so they call themselves cessationists. Now, continuationists believe that the miraculous gifts continue to this day. The problem a cessationist has is that the Bible nowhere indicates that the miraculous gifts cease, and there's so we're constrained. We're constrained by the scriptures as continuationists to believe that when you read First Corinthians twelve and fourteen in particular, and the other gift lists, you find that those gifts are still active today, and there's no indication in the Bible zero that indicates that they are to be. That they're somehow ceasing.

Now, some cessationists will say the 1 Corinthians 13 passage that says, when the perfect comes, then the imperfect passes away. But when you read it in context, they would say that's the Bible. When the perfect comes, that's the Bible, and no longer do we need gifts. You read it in context, you realize that's not the Bible, that's the return of Christ. And so it's a really poor argument. Yeah. And most of the time when you read people who are cessationists, they argue from history and not from the Bible, talking about how gifts have ceased and you can't have records of all these kinds of things. And so that's essentially what a continuationist person is and a cessationist is. And so we would believe in what's called the perpetuity of the gifts, not because that's our preference, but because That's what the Bible says.

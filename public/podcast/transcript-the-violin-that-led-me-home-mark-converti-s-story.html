The Violin That Led Me Home: Mark Converti’s Story of Finding Center Church (part 2)

Sep 17, 2025

Speaker A: This is where it starts now. Break on through.

Here we go.

Rich Richardson: Let it show.

All right, everybody, we're back here with Mark Converti continuing our conversation about his 40 years here, 40-plus years here in our church.

Mark Converti: To be honest, for the next 4 years that it took for me to complete my education, I did not really pursue other Christians. I didn't pursue going to church. I didn't read the Bible. I didn't do any of those spiritual things that you really should do if you are a true believer. Sure. Yeah, right. And so it wasn't until 4 years later, which is now the summer of 1978, that once again God began to do some things in my life. And in particular, I was— at the same time I was studying engineering I was also part of the ASU Symphony Orchestra playing my cello, which is something I'd done since grade school.

It wasn't something I was doing for credit either. It was— music was just kind of a release.

Rich Richardson: Yeah.

Mark Converti: A hobby.

It was like a way to satisfy myself emotionally.

Rich Richardson: Sure, yeah.

Mark Converti: And what I didn't know is that there were people in that orchestra that were saved and that had noticed me and were praying for me.

Rich Richardson: Oh, wow.

Mark Converti: I didn't know that until the end of that summer when I was walking across campus and I ran into a young lady who told me that she was trying to raise money to go to a— what's called a master class in Europe.

She played the violin and the viola, and she wanted to teach anybody who would want to learn to play violin. So she said, you know, if you know anybody, please, please, you know, let me know. So I took her number, went home, didn't think about it much. But a couple of days later, my brother's best friend shows up at my door in the middle of the night holding a violin.

And I thought that was odd because, number one, he doesn't play the violin.

Rich Richardson: Yeah.

Mark Converti: And he told this interesting story about how he was going through the town of Show Low up in northern Arizona.

Rich Richardson: He wasn't on that youth trip, was he?

Mark Converti: No, he was on his own trip.

Rich Richardson: Okay.

Mark Converti: And he happened to pass by a music store. On impulse, he went in, and on another impulse, he bought the violin that was in the window.

Rich Richardson: Wow.

Mark Converti: Just sight unseen. I think he plunked down $300 for it.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Adjusted for inflation, we're talking $2,500.

Mark Converti: That's a lot of money.

Mark Johnson Jr.: In '78?

Mark Converti: Yeah. And, uh, so he had this violin when he came back down to the valley, and he thought, well, you know, it'd be good to know how to play this thing.

Um, and he, he came and visited the only person he knew that knew anything about music, and that was me, and asked if I knew anybody that could teach violin.

Rich Richardson: You're like, in fact, I do.

Mark Converti: As a matter of fact, as I reached in my pocket, pulled out the number that this young lady had given me And called her up.

They arranged for a lesson, which I understand was the only lesson he ever attended, and promptly forgot all about it.

Rich Richardson: Really?

Mark Converti: But that same night, she wanted to talk to me after talking to him, and she asked a very interesting question.

I think I think it went something like, "Hey Mark." Do you go to church?

Nobody had ever asked me that question before.

I said, no, no, I don't right now.

She goes, well, you should come to mine. I think you'd like it.

Okay.

So I took her up on her invitation and The next Sunday, I found myself in what is essentially this church.

Rich Richardson: You you went to Foundation Fellowship.

Mark Converti: I went to Foundation Fellowship, which is what it was called in 1978. 1978, and you've never left. And I've never left.

Mark Johnson Jr.: That's are we is the person that invited you someone we were trying to intentionally not share, or now you made it awkward, Mark. No, I don't know.

Rich Richardson: I don't think we'd know her.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Okay.

Mark Converti: Yeah, you you guys you guys would know her. Okay. But she is somebody who still lives in the Valley, still teaches.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Okay.

Mark Converti: Still is very involved in music and the symphony orchestra that actually I serve in. Wow.

And I've known all these years.

Rich Richardson: Oh, that's great.

Mark Johnson Jr.: It's crazy. I just looked it up for our own curiosity. That spontaneous violin would be the equivalent of $1,500 today.

Rich Richardson: But he never played it? He never learned how to play?

Mark Converti: I think he took the one lesson that she offered him and that was it.

Rich Richardson: Wow.

But I'm glad he stopped in that violin shop because you were invited to the church based on that.

Mark Converti: That's the only reason I came initially anyway. But when I did come, I knew right away that the people assembled there were all doing something and involved in something that I'd never seen before. I could tell that there was something special there, and that something special was the Holy Spirit.

Rich Richardson: Yeah.

Mark Converti: I don't think I knew it at the time, that's what it was, but later I came to understand that I just encountered, you know, the living Christ and the Holy Spirit and God the Father and the assembled saints of born-again believers.

Rich Richardson: Excellent.

Mark Converti: And it wasn't until I met them that I realized that there was more to this faith that I had prayed about 4 years earlier, and that there was some parts of it that were missing. And I think that this is why even today when I hear about somebody that's come to Christ, the very next question I ask them is whether or not they have found a local church.

Rich Richardson: Right.

Mark Converti: A group of believers to belong to. Because it was only until I started to receive teaching from the Scripture that I began to do the other things that accompany salvation, which is baptism.

Rich Richardson: Yep.

Mark Converti: Okay, and receiving the Holy Spirit, which at the time for me was a special event. I actually prayed to receive what we called the baptism of the Holy Spirit at that time. And all the time I was looking at the book of Acts that we had been reading and realizing that these are the steps that the early disciples took. You know, they joined a local church, they received the Holy Spirit, they were baptized, you know, and they began to share life with each other.

Rich Richardson: Right.

Mark Converti: And When I began to experience that, let's call it the fullness of the Gospel, then I began to understand what being a Christian really was. So that kind of is how I got converted.

Rich Richardson: So you would say one of the testimonies that Jesus used is his body testifying to the veracity of Who he is on earth. Yes?

Mark Converti: Yeah.

Rich Richardson: That's powerful.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah.

Mark Converti: Yeah. And so for me, it wasn't an all-in-one thing. You know, I encounter Christ, I walk right into the church, I am baptized. You know, it happened gradually.

So one November day at his apartment swimming pool. Really?

Mark Converti: Yeah.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Wow!

Lynn gave us the history of our church and told us some of the story about what happened to that church foundation fellowship and then how it sort of morphed into us. And so talk about some of those early days from your perspective when you jumped in in '78 and what it was like in the late '70s, early '80s as you were involved in the church.

Mark Converti: Well, you know, to me, the first church I joined, which at the time was called Foundation Fellowship, was a wonderful place to be. The relationships that we had with each other was a very close one. And, you know, I think we really lived out the kind of biblical fellowship that we were always talking about. And we did really share our lives with each other. We bore one another's burdens. We were in each other's houses all the time, eating meals together, serving together, evangelizing together. You know, I was part of the worship team back then.

Mark Johnson Jr.: You are still part of the worship team.

Mark Converti: And I am still part of the worship team. It was wonderful to find out that God had a plan to use music, you know, in the way that he did in the church, which was completely foreign to me at the time. I was classically trained. When I— my first time playing on the worship team, I realized there was no music. Oh yeah? So then how do I know what to play? Well, You play by ear, and that's really what I started to do. And I would say that that the kind of teaching that we received on a regular basis was very solid, very orthodox, very biblically based. I I just ate it up. And I'm very, very grateful for those years of solid teaching from Scripture and really meaningful fellowship with believers.

Rich Richardson: And you— did you live with single guys?

Mark Converti: Yes, I lived in a couple of households. At one point, and I think people would find this a bit amusing today, but At one point, a good half of the church lived on a particular street in Tempe.

Rich Richardson: Yeah.

Mark Converti: Okay.

Rich Richardson: Garfield?

Mark Converti: No, it's called— well, there were 3 streets actually. The one I lived on was Beatrice, and then there was Garfield and a couple of others on either side. And it was kind of interesting. Basically, you could count the houses down from where I lived. And, you know, every, every house had a family that went to our church. Wow. Or was a collection of single guys or single ladies.

Rich Richardson: Yeah.

Mark Converti: That belonged to the church.

Rich Richardson: And where is, where is this? Where is it in Tempe?

Mark Converti: It's in the, it's in the little piece of Tempe that's north of the Salt River.

Rich Richardson: Okay.

Mark Converti: And, um, by the way, I still live there today.

Rich Richardson: You do? Yeah, in your house.

Mark Converti: Not the same house, but a different house.

Rich Richardson: Same area, right?

Mark Converti: Exactly the same neighborhood. Yeah.

Yeah. And so, you know, at that time, we were not only fellowshipping with each other, we were physically living near each other, which some people looked at from the outside and thought maybe we were a cult of some kind. But We weren't. We were just living very close, and being close was very convenient. It meant that you didn't have to, you know, go long distances to go visit somebody. You could just walk a couple houses down.

Rich Richardson: Small group was close.

Mark Converti: Yeah.

Mark Johnson Jr.: You could shoot them a text message or call them on the cell phone, right?

Mark Converti: There were no text messages back then. Maybe.

Mark Johnson Jr.: There were no cell phones back then. Oh yeah. I'm just playing.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Josh, you know.

Mark Converti: So anyway, life went along like that for quite a while, and then somewhere around, I think it was '82, '83, the 3 guys that were leading our church decided that it was not a good idea to be independent. So they went and sought out a ministry that they could be that they could be covered under. Mm-hmm. Where they could be accountable to. And so they went on a nationwide search, which led them to all kinds of places. Eventually, it led to Larry Tomczak and CJ Mahaney's group of churches called Gathering of Believers back then. Now it's called Sovereign Grace Ministries.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Don't skip over the PDI.

Mark Converti: Well, we are on purpose. Yeah, they were called a few other things in the middle, but we're just sticking to the high points. We're sticking to that for simplicity's sake.

Rich Richardson: Thank you. Yeah, yeah. And, um, but our association with that, uh, with that group of churches and with those leaders did cause change to occur in us. The biggest change that occurred, I think, was that accountability piece that I mentioned earlier, which is something the guys sought, but I don't think they were prepared for the effects of accountability. It's one thing to say you want it. It's another thing to get it, right?

Mark Converti: And so, again, you'll have to go back to Lynn's podcast to find out what happened.

Rich Richardson: Yeah, he tells the whole story there.

Mark Converti: I'm not going to reiterate it here, but suffice it to say that at a certain point, we realized that we could not go forward the way that we were currently constituted or built, that a change was needed. That's what caused the eventual split in our church. And a large group of people stayed, and a smaller group left and stayed with what is now Sovereign Grace Ministries.

Rich Richardson: Yeah.

Mark Converti: And that was in 1986.

Rich Richardson: Now, thrown into the middle of all that, And if you're listening to this podcast and you've been through challenges with any kind of church or division or whatever, division is one of the hardest things I think a Christian ever has to go through when it comes to church. Would you agree with that, Mark?

Mark Converti: I would. It was a painful time.

Rich Richardson: Yeah, and you have friends that leave and don't treat you like friends, and you have men and women that you respect and maybe have mentored you or discipled you or whatever that act differently than they talk to you about. And so how did you navigate that time?

Mark Converti: Well, um, you know, this, this split that I'm talking about that occurred was not a split over, uh, things like moral problems. You know, uh, there was no unfaithfulness. There was no Blatant sin.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah, no disqualification or anything.

Mark Converti: No, it wasn't anything like that. It was more a question of style of ministry and maybe some points of theology. And it just came to a point where the senior pastor of the church wanted to go one way and the rest of the people in leadership wanted to go another. And for me, it became a simple choice of— not a simple choice, but a difficult choice, really, of deciding who was the right— what was the right direction to go. And for me, it became— it came down to this, that I was wanting to stay with the people Who were the most forward-looking of the group, the ones that were leading us in the direction of a more well-defined and purposeful theology and style of ministry. Amen. I saw that to be the Sovereign Grace guys.

Rich Richardson: Yeah.

Mark Converti: And why? Well, because up until then, through the teaching that these folks had brought us, uh, and the, the various kinds of ministry that they, they brought— because there were times when we gathered with them and we had these meetings called Celebrations, which were Kind of like multiple church gatherings, I began to realize that these guys were really kind of far ahead of where we were spiritually. Yeah. I thought they were more respectful of and more glued to the Scriptures than we were, that we tended to be more experiential in our worship, and that they were more, I guess you could say, orthodox.

Rich Richardson: Sure.

Mark Converti: And this was a difference that could be noticed because, you know, we could all individually search the Scriptures to find out if what was being preached was true, which, by the way, is what Scripture itself advocates that we do.

Rich Richardson: Yeah, right.

Mark Converti: And the more I saw the direction they were going, the more I saw it lined up with Scripture, and that's why I made the choice that I did.

Rich Richardson: Gotcha.

Mark Converti: You know, I probably— that's probably a little bit simplistic, but when it gets right down to it, that's, that's the reason I made the choice that I did. to go with what seemed to be the unpopular choice.

Rich Richardson: Yeah, because it was the least, the smallest group.

Mark Converti: It was the smallest group.

Rich Richardson: Right, because the way I understand it, the church Foundation Fellowship was something like 700 people.

Mark Converti: Yep.

Rich Richardson: And how many stayed with the senior, former senior pastor?

Mark Converti: At least 2/3.

Mark Converti: And all the assets, yeah.

Mark Converti: 150 maybe.

Mark Converti: Yeah, we went to— we met at a hotel for a couple of weeks. And then we went to a school, a much smaller school than the place that we were renting out when we were all together as Foundation Fellowship. And so we were leaving behind a lot if you were looking at the quantity of assets or the quantity of people.

Rich Richardson: Sure.

Mark Converti: But like I say, what I saw in the people that I ended up following was a group of people that were going on. They were progressing on with where I thought I saw the Holy Spirit going.

Rich Richardson: Yeah.

Mark Converti: And again, much more adherence to the teaching of Scripture. So that's how I made my choice. And yeah, so it was a difficult time because this division that you spoke of, this split, did split people right down the middle. Family members.

Rich Richardson: Yeah.

Mark Converti: I know a couple of families where, you know, half the family went one way, the other half the other way. Goodness. Um, certainly among my friends, people my age at the time, uh, you know, ours was a much smaller group. Let's put it that way.

Mark Converti: Yeah.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Right.

Mark Converti: That was difficult to do.

Rich Richardson: Yeah.

Mark Converti: Or to try to make it something that it wasn't. Like I said, this wasn't due to widespread moral failure. It was more of a theological difference or a style of ministry difference.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah.

Mark Converti: Than it was any of those other things. And so it took a while, I think, for that to kind of settle out. And I would say it took a lot longer than most of us expected it to.

Mark Converti: Because it didn't just end with 2 churches meeting at different places. Both groups, both camps, if you will, went through additional difficulties. Yes.

What Is The Bridge Course?

Jul 16, 2025

Rich Richardson: This is... Fixed on Jesus.

Rich Richardson: Hey everybody, welcome back to the Fixed on Jesus podcast of Center Gilbert, Arizona. My name is Rich, and joining me as always is Mark Johnson Jr.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Hello, Rich.

Rich Richardson: Hey, Mark. And then Heather Corbett behind the glass.

Speaker A: Hi guys.

Rich Richardson: Mark, today we're talking about— I'm going to do the interviewing this time, so.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Oh wow.

Rich Richardson: Yeah, today we're talking about the Bridge Course coming up.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yep.

Rich Richardson: So maybe tell us a little bit about the Bridge Course. What is it? Sure. And why should we care?

Mark Johnson Jr.: Sure. Yeah. So it's a course designed by our sister church, particularly Jim Donahue, geared toward evangelism and apologetics. So it's a course that any church can put on for free.

Rich Richardson: But our church is putting it on?

Mark Johnson Jr.: Our church is putting it on, yes. I guess I was just going to explain what it is in general and then how it might matter.

Rich Richardson: Maybe what it applies to us.

Mark Johnson Jr.: It applies to us because we've ran it once last year in 2024.

Mark Johnson Jr.: And we, it was 8 weeks long and we had about 20 people attend and probably close to 40 people serving. So if you're listening to this and you served in the Bridge Course, thank you so much. We couldn't have done it without you. Um, we still have some people in the church that found the church through the Bridge Course. So that was a huge blessing.

Rich Richardson: That's cool.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah. So, so just overarching, in an overarching way, what is the goal of the Bridge Course? To get people to church? The goal is to lead people to Christ.

Rich Richardson: Oh, okay.

Mark Johnson Jr.: To get them saved too.

Rich Richardson: So it's a presentation of the Gospel.

Mark Johnson Jr.: It is. It's a little even more than that. So now is it like, are you teaching? No, no, I am not capable or trained to do that. But our good friend Jim Donahue— So we fly him in and do it or what? No, we can't afford that either.

Rich Richardson: Okay. How do we do this? Yeah. How do we do it?

Mark Johnson Jr.: Good question. I'm at a loss and told. He made these videos that give about a 30-minute presentation on a specific objection to Christianity. And then after that, a guest would be able to discuss it amongst their table and ask questions. And we'll have people serving as table leaders where they're primarily responsible for inviting and bringing people, whether it's coworkers, neighbors, relatives, Anyone, anyone who's interested, who has questions. So walk us through a night. Somebody comes in, they come to a building, come to our building. What do they expect? What's first? Good question.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Well, first is probably me pacing around in the foyer area, welcoming them and trying not to scare them off because I'm very intimidating sometimes.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Or maybe Steve Tong or someone like him would be there greeting. and saying hello, and they'll receive a booklet that says, it's titled, How Good Are You? And on that booklet, it kind of walks them through the Gospel if they want to read it on their own time. They'll also get a free Bible. And I know they're not as excited about that as they would be about the food. So— Ah, so there's food. There's food.

Rich Richardson: So we serve them dinner?

Mark Johnson Jr.: That's correct. Dinner and dessert.

Rich Richardson: Okay.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah. So every week, all 10 weeks in a row, There's food. And so they'll start off the night by finding a table and someone will walk them to the table.

Rich Richardson: Yeah. Right.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah. And then the food starts at 6:30 and a gracious group of members from our church prepare all the food and— Yeah, they do a great job. Do a great job. It's definitely a highlight of the course. I wouldn't even want to run the course if we didn't have food, to be honest with you.

Rich Richardson: It's a big draw, yeah.

Mark Johnson Jr.: So during mealtime, they'll get a chance to just kind of chat, or they don't have to, but there's an opportunity to talk with people at their table. Or most people who came last year came with someone they knew, or they knew someone there. They're connected to someone somehow. And that makes it a little bit easier of a transition for some of these folks who have never been in a church. I remember the first time I walked into a church and it was scary.

Rich Richardson: It's weird. Yeah, right. So we really, the heart is just to be hospitable and loving to them about it. So anyways, back to night of experience. They'll get their food, they'll sit down, they'll have a chat for 20 minutes or so. I'll come up, say a really bad, corny dad joke. Yeah, something along those lines. Right in your wheelhouse.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah, right in my wheelhouse. That's right. Maybe I'll do some singing. Who knows?

Rich Richardson: No, you shouldn't do that.

Mark Johnson Jr.: I probably shouldn't.

Rich Richardson: Keep them coming back.

Mark Johnson Jr.: And then I'll put on a video. And the title of the first video is How Good Are You? And there's nine others like it. They'll listen to the video. They're about thirty minutes long. Then they'll take a quick break, use the restroom, do whatever you have to do, and then we'll serve usually coffee and dessert. And then a short table discussion will will start with their table on the video. No, the video will be over.

Rich Richardson: No, no, no.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Oh yes, on the video, but not just limited to the video.

Rich Richardson: So whatever they want to talk pretty much.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah, yeah. I mean.

Rich Richardson: So that topic, how good are you, is that like, do most people come in thinking that they're good enough to be able to go to heaven because they haven't murdered anybody, haven't killed any, haven't done anything really horrible? And so— Absolutely. And the video, what does the video communicate?

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah, the video uses a really good analogy on a scale of Hitler being at the bottom, Mother Teresa being at the top. How good are you? And almost everyone says upper middle. I'm not as— I'm not nearly as bad as Hitler, but I'm not as good as Mother Teresa, but I'm a little bit below that.

Rich Richardson: Yeah, right.

Mark Johnson Jr.: So the idea is that if that's the scale, how good then do you have to be to go to heaven?

Rich Richardson: Right.

Mark Johnson Jr.: So, and that's just a common thought that the culture or a non-Christian, including myself, had before getting saved.

Rich Richardson: Absolutely.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah, right. My good deeds outweigh my bad.

Rich Richardson: Yeah, I'm good. I'm fine.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah, I'm not Hitler.

Rich Richardson: No, I'm not racist. I'm not mean to my family. I don't beat anybody up. Right.

Mark Johnson Jr.: I'm good.

Rich Richardson: Right.

Mark Johnson Jr.: So, I don't know. I've heard it expressed where you kind of have to hear the bad news before you hear the good news.

Rich Richardson: Right.

Mark Johnson Jr.: And the bad news is that we're all headed towards hell without any— No one is good. No one is good. Yeah. Not one. Romans 3 tells us. Yeah. So then what time does the night end? I mean, some people might think, you know, sometimes I've heard stories about churches Locking doors and like keeping people hostage? When does it end? At a certain time or what?

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah, that's a good question. We're very, very stern on an 8— hard 8:15 done. 8:15, I'm going up there and I'm saying, you don't have to go home, but you can't stay here. And I just say that and everyone scatters.

Rich Richardson: Okay.

Mark Johnson Jr.: No, but yeah, seriously, they, they don't, they don't have to. We don't want them to stay. And Actually, the way the videos teach the table leaders are in a fashion that wants to leave them wanting to discuss more, rather than pummeling them with the truth so much that they never want to come back. So one of the common questions I'll get about the course is, do I have to come to all of them? And the answer is no. You'll hear the gospel in each individual video and at each table discussion. But it is recommended that you come because a lot of them handle or talk about a lot of the major objections to Christianity. A couple of the others are, why doesn't God forgive everyone? How can God allow suffering? Which is probably the most common one that people ask, right?

Mark Johnson Jr.: How could God send people to hell? Yeah. So they really hit the nail on the head with some of the common objections. But the good news is that none of these objections are new, and most of them have been answered.

Rich Richardson: But at the same time, as we interact with unbelievers in our family or in our neighborhood or in our work or school, sometimes it's hard to answer all those questions.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Right, right.

Rich Richardson: And so this is a good outlet. I mean, it's not a substitution for us evangelizing people in our own lives, but It is a tool we can use to invite people who might have questions, right?

Mark Johnson Jr.: Exactly. Yeah. It takes some of the heavy lifting off of you theologically. So think of it like a program that we run. It's not the only way to do it, but it's a pretty good time-tested way to do it. And they'll get a— your guest that you invite will get a clear gospel presentation. They'll get to hear What it takes to be safe. They'll get to hear some answers to some of the historical objections to Christianity, and but most importantly—not most importantly—but importantly—they'll get hospitable, loving people being kind to them, serving them a meal.

Mark Johnson Jr.: People often stay late and hang out after.

Rich Richardson: Yeah, just talking because they enjoy it. They get—they get to know other people.

Mark Johnson Jr.: To know other people. Yeah.

Mark Johnson Jr.: So I would say the main need, if you're listening to this as a church member, is invite people, start inviting them now. The first one is on Tuesday, April 7th at 6:30 PM. Either now or soon in the future, we'll have little invite cards.

Rich Richardson: Okay.

Mark Johnson Jr.: On those invite cards, it has a little QR code that gives a 2-minute explanation of what the course is for you or your potential guests could listen.

Mark Johnson Jr.: It's something we just— one of the really cool things that happened is it really ministered to the people that we're serving.

Rich Richardson: Yeah.

Mark Johnson Jr.: So the people who listen to the videos might not have heard a clear explanation on some of the topics that Jim addresses in the videos. So, I mean, it was successful in the way that so many people serve, the gospel went forward, we were hospitable to guests, and we were able to be strengthened by God's word in the form of Jim's presentation.

Rich Richardson: Sure. So when is the first Bridge Course night?

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah, the first one is on Tuesday, April 7th at 6:30 PM. And then it goes on 10 weeks after that, 10 weeks total. I know that you're listening to this. It's probably July or August depending on when this releases. And we're talking about this early because we know it's really important to build relationships and aim toward inviting guests. It's a very relational style of evangelism and apologetics. And we would just love for you to bring your neighbors, your guests, coworkers, people that you're friends with, family members. All are welcome and we'd love to have you.

Rich Richardson: Okay. Now, if somebody wants to find out more about what's being taught at the Bridge Course, is there a website?

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah. Yep. bridgecourse.org. You'll see, you can even watch the videos. An intro video that isn't included in our schedule that you can watch on there if you'd like. Yeah, you'll see more and more about it at the church if you haven't already, whether it's just an explanation of what the course is, tips on how you can invite people. I guess I can tell you a story of how I invited someone for this past year. They didn't show up. I invited someone again. We'll see if they show up. I mean, if you really think about it, we're close to 400 members, including kids.

Mark Johnson Jr.: If everyone invited one person, that's 400 people that got invited. If 10% of those come up, that's 40 people. That's double what we had last year. So I just urge you to step out in faith knowing that the Lord can do something, and we have a truth that's so good that we don't want to keep that from someone.

Rich Richardson: Amen. So how, like, how can maybe somebody who's afraid to, to, uh, to invite someone, like, what tips do you have?

Mark Johnson Jr.: Sure. I would say the way this is set up, it's, it's relational. So not, not to say that relational evangelism is the only way to do it, but I don't think— let's start with what not to do. I don't think you should go stand on the street corner and hand these cards out to people you've never talked with. I think you should approach people you know, people you work with. Hey, Rich, I know that you've asked me a couple questions about Christianity in the past, and you know that I'm involved at my local church. I really don't have the answers that you're looking for, but my church does this program that is open to everyone. We serve you a meal and you can come in and I'll be there too. I'm happy to go with you if that makes you feel more comfortable.

Mark Johnson Jr.: But you can come and hear what the core understanding of Christianity is. And it's a really low-pressure environment. You won't be called on. You don't have to share anything.

Rich Richardson: You don't have to know anything. You don't have to know anything. It's free. You get food. You'll be able to ask whatever questions you want. There's really nothing that's off limits. And we would want to understand what you believe too. It's just a safe place to share. If now there's no pressure to come, like I know life's busy. I don't want to.

Rich Richardson: There's no expectation. But here's the invite card that has all the info. But from now until then, if you have any questions, you can ask me or and if you want to check out the videos, they're on bridgecourse.org. So we'd love to have you. My church is really hospitable, and honestly, the food's just really good too. Yeah. And it's not that much of a commitment if it's 6:30 to 8:15 once a week, really.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah.

Rich Richardson: You know, it's not even 2 hours. And so, you know, that's good. And I think it's also like maybe there are people I've heard of, and I think you told me about this, that are playing the videos in their homes.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah.

Rich Richardson: And inviting their friends and neighbors around to come and watch them and like have dessert or coffee.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah.

Rich Richardson: Coffee, right?

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah, these videos fit in any context. I mean, we're doing them at a church, but I could even make a case that they might work better in a home because it's more familiar and it's more family-oriented.

Rich Richardson: Yeah, that makes sense.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah, but either way, you'll get to hear a strong gospel presentation. It's something that brought the church together to serve. It was just really cool to be a part of it.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Heather was there too. She helped me fight off the demons that were attacking our videos.

Mark Johnson Jr.: But yeah, it was fun.

Mark Johnson Jr.: I think it was 40 people who served, which is a huge blessing.

Mark Johnson Jr.: What do you got, Heather?

Speaker A: Yeah, I really would like to stress the part that it's very low pressure and the environment is very friendly. So that no question is off limits and people can truly ask the hard questions that, I mean, I've had great opportunities to sit down with Rich and ask questions and he's answered them. Not everybody has opportunity to do that 'cause they don't belong to such a great church like we do. Yeah.

Mark Johnson Jr.: But here's an opportunity where you can sit down and ask a question and you don't feel like, I'm an idiot, or some other terrible thing that they've heard probably before for not understanding or whatever. So I really enjoyed that part about it because it was so comfortable. Yeah.

Speaker A: Yeah.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Oh yeah. And that's the goal. We're not trying to pressure anyone. We're not selling anything. We just feel that we were redeemed, bought with a price, saved by the grace of God. That truth is just too good not to share. If you think someone's going to hell, which if they don't know Christ, the Bible says they are, the most loving thing you can do is to tell them the Gospel. And if you don't feel confident or competent to share the Gospel, you can bring them to the Bridge Course. You can do both. You should do both.

Mark Johnson Jr.: You should share the Gospel.

Rich Richardson: Exactly.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Invite them. You can feed them.

Mark Johnson Jr.: I mean, there was there was a guy last year who came in angry, like mad at the Lord. Really just wanted to debate, and that's fine. I don't know the status of his soul. He only came a couple times, but I'm happy. I was happy he was there because that might have been the only time he heard the gospel, and you never know what can happen. Like it's not up to us to save people. We're called to step out in faith and invite people and tell them the gospel. We're not called to make results happen. That's not that's not what we're called to do. We're called to scatter seed.

Mark Johnson Jr.: The Lord causes the growth, not us. And we want to be outward facing as a church inward upward and outward.

Rich Richardson: That's right.

Mark Johnson Jr.: All three, not just one or the other. Not just inward, not just upward.

Rich Richardson: Yep.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Outward as well. And we we're trying to be obedient through this program and and others.

Rich Richardson: Yeah, that's good. Wow! Praise God for that. And I'm thrilled that you've stepped up and taken leadership and done a really— you did a great job last year and you're gonna do a great job this year. And I'm excited to hear the testimonies that'll come from it. So if anyone has questions, you can email us at production@senategilbert.com. If you know Mark, know what he looks like, you can talk to him on Sunday.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah.

Rich Richardson: And if you don't know what he looks like and you know what I look like, I can point you to him. And, uh, but yeah, it's a, it's a great, it's a great experience, a great environment. Uh, it's really what it's all about, just to share the gospel of Jesus Christ to people who, who don't know. Um, in, in years past, we did something similar, and there are people who got saved, baptized, and are now members of our church. And so, uh, and you know, that's what we want to see. We want to see people impacted forever for the Lord, and this is how that can happen.

Mark Johnson Jr.: Yeah, I love it. Yeah, if you have any anything you want to ask me, whether it's how to invite questions about Bridge Course, pull me aside. I'm here every Sunday. If you don't know me, like Rich, then ask him. But we'd love to just yeah. I don't know. I just have a passion. I want to see people get saved, and it's not our job to cause a revival, but we can step out in faith, and I'm just excited about it. So glad for the opportunity.

Rich Richardson: Amen. All right, well, thanks for leading. See you, everybody.

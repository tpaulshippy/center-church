Stories have this way of captivating our minds and our hearts. There's all kinds of stories out there. There's epics. There are dramas. There are comedies and love stories.

And all stories are told from a certain perspective or what's called a narrative. Narratives exist in every form of media, be it the news or social media, maybe it's TikTok. Whatever it is that you can consume in the form of media, narratives abound. They're everywhere.

For example, the Ariana Grande song I quoted the lyrics from contained a narrative. It was Grande's narrative of a breakup.

It was her version of how she felt, what she experienced, and how she longs for the story to have a different outcome.

Narratives, they can serve a purpose. They can be good.

They can help us to better understand the truth.

When a witness testifies at a trial, what they're doing is they're giving their narrative of a situation or of the events that occurred leading up to or during a crime. All with the hopes that their testimony might lead to the conviction of the suspect or maybe even their exoneration.

In the beginning of John's epistle, we are given a narrative. What we just read. It's a narrative. The Apostle John, the same John who wrote the Gospel of John, the three letters that bear his name, and the book of Revelation is giving us an eyewitness account. A narrative of a story.

Now, being that this is from John's perspective, this story begins pretty oddly. He begins it by saying, literally, what was from the beginning, what we have heard, what we have seen with our eyes, and so on. Why is this odd? Well, how many of you have ever received a letter that began with, what was from the beginning, what I've heard, what I've seen with my eyes? I'm sure not many of us.

But it also begins differently than any other epistle in the New Testament.

There's no address, there's no audience that he's addressing. What John does is he begins with a series of statements with relative pronouns to describe his experience. Now, a little English 101, a relative pronoun is a word that distinguishes or clarifies the noun that it's modifying. So, when he begins with what was from the beginning, we're supposed to wonder, what's the noun that he is modifying? Who is the what or what is the what? To put it in layman's terms, we're left wondering what exactly was from the beginning.

Well, John gives us some clues. He writes, what we've heard, what we've seen with our eyes, what we've looked upon and have touched with our hands concerning the word of life.

These are tangible experiences. The clues are to be found not in the nouns, but in the verbs.

Bet you didn't think that you were going to get an English lesson this morning, but a noun names a person, place, or thing, right? And a verb, what does it do? It describes an action, an occurrence, or a state of being.

There are a few verbs in this text. He hears, he looks upon, and he touches.

This implies that the what John is calling attention to is actually a who.

It was someone, and this someone was real. Real enough to be heard, real enough to be seen, real enough to be touched. John wraps up this first verse by saying this someone was the word of life.

Now he chooses a very interesting way to introduce us to the main character of the story. He calls him the word of life.

This is meant to draw our attention somewhere else.

It's meant to point to another story that John wrote, and that was his gospel. The beginning of John's gospel which is a narrative about the events of the life of Jesus, John writes, in the beginning was the word. And just a little bit after that he writes, in him was life. The word of life. Clearly, this someone John is saying who was heard, seen, and touched was none other than Jesus Christ.

The word John uses then for beginning in verse 1, it's an echo. It's echoing something that was actually from the very beginning. Genesis 1-1 where it says, in the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. It's an intentional echo. He places it there for a purpose.

It's also an intentional echo of his gospel. In John 1-1 where it says, in the beginning was the word. Three different places where he talks about in the beginning and he's referring to who was from the beginning? Jesus.

Jesus always was. He's always existed. There's never a moment Jesus wasn't. Now, this doesn't, probably doesn't sound like a monumental truth to you. There's some huge moment where a light bulb goes off. That's okay. But we do need to allow it to sink in.

Then John mentions the word fellowship in verse 3 and he makes the case that his own fellowship is with this always loving and existing Father and his always loving and existing Son, Jesus Christ.

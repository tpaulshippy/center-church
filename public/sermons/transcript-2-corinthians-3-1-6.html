2 Corinthians 3:1-6

Rich Richardson · Apr 28, 2024

There is NO CONTAINING the Holy Spirit, HE alone works life altering change.

Please, please plan on coming. All right, if you have a Bible, please open up to 2 Corinthians.

2 Corinthians is in the New Testament.

Coincidentally, it comes after 1 Corinthians. We're going to be in 2 Corinthians. We've heard of what's called a Sisyphean task. According to Greek mythology, Sisyphus was punished by the gods, and his punishment was that he was condemned to roll a huge stone up a hill, only always to have that stone rolled down the same hill. And so he was condemned to do this task over and over and over and over. Therefore, a Sisyphean task is a task impossible to complete. Not hard, but impossible. And so today, in 2 Corinthians 3, verses 1 through 6, we are going to talk about, or Paul's going to show us, the ultimate Sisyphean task. What is that? Effective Christian ministry.

Effective Christian ministry is impossible on our own. And you might think, well, I'm not a pastor. This doesn't apply to me. That's not true. We as Christians are all called to full-time ministry. Only a handful of us are set aside for full-time pastoral ministry. All of us are called to be ministers of the gospel, both to believers and unbelievers alike. And that task, friends, Paul's going to show us, is impossible on our own. It's impossible on our own to pray and expect anything to happen.

Or to counsel and expect anyone to change. Or to preach and expect it to do any good. Or to evangelize and expect someone to get saved. Or to confront someone and expect them to be convicted. Or to have a Bible study and expect someone to hear from God. Or to attend a small group and expect someone to benefit. Or to fellowship and expect someone to be encouraged. Or to share the gospel and expect anyone to believe. All of these things are impossible Sisyphean tasks without the active spirit of God.

This is what the former professor of homiletics, which is preaching at Trinity Evangelical Divinity School, illustrated when he took his seminary class on a field trip to one of the local cemeteries. He says it this way. I take them, these students, to a little cemetery in Deerfield, Illinois. And I have them gather around a certain gravesite. I point out the name. And then I tell one of the students, preach the gospel to Mr. Smith here.

They look at me like I'm nuts. So I preach to Mr. Smith with enthusiasm. Sir, Jesus died for your sins. You must put your faith in him. Then I look at the students and I tell them, there is no difference than preaching the gospel to unsaved people. The Bible says that they are dead in their sins. You can preach your heart out, but nothing will happen unless God does a miracle to give them life to listen.

Christian ministry is impossible without the action of the Holy Spirit. It's impossible for us without the activity of the Spirit to experience the gift of conviction or encouragement.

To do any good on our own is completely and totally impossible. This is why we take very seriously what Jesus says in John 15, 15, 5. I am the vine. You are the branches. Whoever abides in me and I in him, he it is who bears much fruit. For apart from me you can do nothing. Nothing.

So we want to do things not apart from him. So it stands to reason that we must understand how to read the signs of the work of the Spirit of God. We are all called to minister. But we all need to recognize that we never minister alone. And more than that, we are utterly and completely reliant on the Holy Spirit's power and presence to do any good. Anything that comes from any enduring value comes primarily not from us, but the work of the Spirit. So how can we tell when the Spirit of God is working?

Today we are going to learn how to do that. Paul is going to take us by the hand and show us how to discern the Spirit's work.

Why does this matter?

If you don't know how to understand how the Spirit works, you might think that biblical success is just when people say the right things or gather in large numbers. You might think the Holy Spirit is active when people cry or laugh or are influential in society. You might think that the Holy Spirit is active when you get all kinds of clicks and likes or people are especially theologically precise or are emotionally invested. You might even think the Spirit is active when people pray or raise their hand or come down an aisle. But none of those things, none of those things in and of themselves are surefire signs of the Spirit's work. You can say all the right things, believe all the right things, and yet not be connected to the Holy Spirit. So we have to recognize the sign of the Spirit's work. What is that sign?

In a word, it's change. It's real, lasting, life-altering change. This change that starts on the inside but never stays on the inside. Why? Because there's no containing the Holy Spirit. He alone works life-altering change. So we need to know what this looks like. And we're going to see this in 2 Corinthians 3, verses 1-6.

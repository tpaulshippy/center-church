2 Corinthians 4:7-15

Rich Richardson · May 26, 2024

Success for Christians is the life dependent on Jesus.

If you have your Bible with you, we're going to be in 2 Corinthians 4, starting at verse 7 this morning.

In 1930, the timeless lesson taught by author Waddy Piper and his "Little Engine That Could" has been an important part of nearly every American's childhood. If you're all like me, you can recall the lesson designed to convey confidence, hard work, and determination. In many senses, it was our first dose of the American dream and work ethic. We can achieve whatever we want if we work hard enough and stick to it. What do we do, though, when we learn that the engine that could does not look like life? What do you do when our train ride looks completely different? When all the friendly animals that are cheering on the side are missing? The big, smiling sun in the sky is covered by clouds and all the people in the train cars behind you are complaining about the food and asking when you are going to be more like the fancy train on the tracks next to you?

What do you do? What do you do when you are just about to run out of gas and have barely started to head up the infamous hill whose peak is hidden by the rain clouds? Do you ignore the fuel gauge and start chanting, "I think I can! I think I can! I think I..." Or, in the crowding of opposition, do you look to grab the mic, look in the mirror at those in your car, and say something like, "I'm sorry, folks. I just can't do it. It's too much. I'm not strong enough. I'm almost out of gas and the hill's just too big." Last week, we were reminded that the centrality of the Gospel is the defining detail that makes Christian ministry unique in our world. Today, we will see how the success of that ministry is accomplished through His weak, broken, bloodied, and opposed people.

Let's turn to the text. 2 Corinthians 4, starting at verse 7. "But we have this treasure in jars of clay, to show that the surpassing power belongs to God and not to us. We are afflicted in every way, but not crushed. Perplexed, but not driven to despair. Persecuted, but not forsaken. Struck down, but not destroyed. Always carrying in the body the death of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus may also be manifested in our bodies. For we who live are always being given over to death for Jesus' sake, so that the life of Jesus also may be manifested in our mortal flesh. So death is at work in us, but life in you." "Please pray with me." "The Spirit of God, we ask that you would help us this morning." "It is amazing to think that you know, in the same way you know me and all that goes on in my heart, and know all of my circumstances, all of my situations, you know that of everyone here." "And so I pray somehow that this man would disappear and your word would be heard.

And I pray you would work through it in all of our hearts. Help us to see Jesus in his name. Amen." You will recall last week that Paul clarified his life in ministry by stating in the verse before that we proclaim not ourselves, but Christ, Jesus Christ, as Lord. With ourselves as your servants for Jesus' sake. What our text says this morning is tightly hitched to this defining statement. Paul wants the church in Corinth and us to know that his ministry and our ministry measure success in a completely different way than we're used to seeing. Success is not measured by followers. It is not measured by likes or upvotes. It is not measured by subscriptions or revenue. It is not measured by campuses or degrees.

It is not measured by publishing deals, neighborhoods, or having your best life now. Success for Paul, success in the Bible for Christians is being dependent on Jesus. This may not seem like a prolific statement, but as it was for the Corinthians, the gravity of this comes from our overabundance of bad measuring and poor math. When we're honest with ourselves, we have a big problem with the idea of something like dependence. We don't like it. It's not American. We celebrate independence. We chase lifestyles off the grid. We don't like to be tied down or committed. We prefer to hold our options open and make our own decisions.

What's the reason for this? We want to have control over as much of our lives as possible. We want to know that we will get the appropriate outcome if we work hard enough. This is where stories like the little engine that could, they come from this place. They are designed to reinforce the idea that we have all we need within ourselves. And as long as we realize that ability, there is no obstacle big enough to stop us. We have no need to be dependent on anyone or anything. It is completely counterintuitive to count success based on dependence. It's foreign and it leaves us confused because if we're honest, dependence communicates weakness. And for the church in Corinth, weakness was like a curse.

It was a source of shame and a target of ridicule, much like dependence now in our own day. Knowing this personally though, Paul's approach to the Corinthian church is not to teach them a lesson on how much better or stronger he was and he was. Instead, he slows down and he shows us all the true measurement and standard of success. He starts this by reminding us where the real power in ministry and in life comes from. He says we have this treasure in jars of clay to show that the surpassing power belongs to who? To God and not to us. It might seem like an odd statement, but this analogy would have not been lost on that church, on the Corinthian church. In a world where there are countless pots and jars and other clay containers, a statement like this screams loud and clear, ordinary and plain.

It would be something like Paul comparing himself to a plastic sandwich bag. This is hardly the kind of thing that someone would want to put anything of value in. Not only are they often indistinguishable from one another, but they are brittle and easily destroyed. It is no mistake that Corinth, of course, was known not only for clay pots, but being a place where very elaborate, high-end bronze vessels and pots were made. You know, something more fitting and distinguished for the mentioned treasure that Paul says. Paul, though, what he wants, he wants us to see the significance of this brittle-natured and ordinary device as being uniquely positioned in a way that no other vessel could be. Because all of the attention goes to the treasure and not to the jar. And if you've ever been around a playground or a school or, frankly, any of my cars, you will see evidence of this because they are typically covered with sandwich bags.

You see, these sandwich bags, kids will take them and hold them and they have their goldfish or their sandwich or Cheez-Its or whatever. And the moment it's empty, the moment it ceases to hold the snack, they lose this, it's amazing how this happens. They suddenly lose the ability to hold it anymore. And it falls and there it remains until it clogs my vacuum. When Paul tells us we are expendable as ordinary containers, he does so that in our ordinariness, all of our worth, all of our significance is not wrapped up in our ability to look good or have a great personality or have immeasurable talent or be highly educated or be incredible with finances. It is 110% dependent on the contents it serves to hold and that alone. He tells us this much when he says this is not just a coincidence, but it's on purpose.

So that we can know something we didn't know before. That we are not where the real power comes from. Not in ministry and not in life. The real power comes from God. It is at this point that you can almost imagine the Corinthians receiving this and hearing it or reading it for the first time and then rolling their eyes just like your teenager. And being a little annoyed. Come on, Paul. Really? Of course the power comes from God. That doesn't mean you can't put a little effort.

You know, try a little bit. Maybe be more of a brash jar. A little talent and strength never hurt anyone after all. So when he continues, when he pushes this further, he begins to explain what it's like to be a treasure carrying sandwich bag. He says, "We are afflicted in every way but not crushed. We're perplexed but not driven to despair. Persecuted but not forsaken. Struck down but not destroyed." Now it's not super easy to see this there, but when Paul lists these things, he does so in a way that he conveys a present ongoing activity. It's sort of the ancient way of moving through cutaway scenes, where the audience is meant to understand these, not as a once upon a time this happened, but these are situations that Paul is currently experiencing. Maybe more like, we are being oppressed in every way right now, but we are not being crushed.

We are currently very confused, but we are not being driven to despair. Or, we are persecuted as we speak, but have not been left alone. Or, we are being pummeled to the ground, however, we are not currently destroyed. If you're like me, when you hear this, you might think, "Oh, okay, Paul, great. Glad you're sticking with it. Keep toughing it out." As though Paul is talking about digging deep to overcome adversity or something like that. But Paul is taking the beating for a lifetime. He is getting whooped in every which way. Left, right, up, and down from every angle. And from all sorts of enemies in all sorts of ways.

And truthfully, he should be absolutely wiped off the planet at this point. However, that's not the case. He is still going against all odds and against his nature, ability, and talents. In fact, Paul is not just calling himself a brittle clay jar. He is completely ruling himself out as the source of anything at all except for pain and failure. And although the opposition is aggressively oppressing him, God keeps him from being crushed. Although he does not understand, God guards him against despair. Though he is persecuted, God's presence is clear to him. Though he is beaten down, God does not permit him to be destroyed. And though, in all of this, he sees very clearly the true source of power is not Paul.

It is God. If we're honest with ourselves, we can get on board with something like this. That is, until we ourselves face the test of elasticity. We think that we're all like Rocky was against Ivan Drago in Rocky IV at the end of the movie. You know, when he just gets whooped on. It's like he's totally going to take one. And then at that point, we think, yeah, just like Rocky, I'm going to dig deep and I'll get up and that will be the end. I'll win the day. But none of us feel that way if we're honest. None of us feel that comeback.

None of us sense that rising up against all odds when our worst fears call at three in the morning. We don't feel so self-sufficient when our marriages show significant cracks on the way to the party. How equipped do we feel when there's no more money and everyone is hungry at home? How often are we in ourselves when the kids you have just given your life to for 20 plus years are now moved out and gone and you don't know what you're going to do with yourself? What makes these circumstances difficult is not that they come, but that they almost always come at the worst possible time. They occupy a space in our lives that is almost always crowded with a million other things that have to be done. The kinds of things that people are actually counting on you for. And the truth is that these and countless other situations are there to show us, to help us to see that we are nothing more than sandwich bags.

Or as Paul says, clay pots that are just easily broken, completely ordinary and easily replaced. But in his provision, these images of clay pots do much more than humble us. They are used to show that God's power is for us. Over the short period of time that I've been a part of this eldership, I have seen how unexpected phone calls, emergency meetings, emergency room visits have almost always been in the context of a hundred other things going on. As anyone who wants to help, you look forward to being there for people in their moment of need and providing them with some sense of stability when things feel wobbly all around them. But what really ends up happening is you get the call when you least expect it. You find out you're needed and you spend the whole drive trying to figure out which way is up.

It is uniquely in our dependence that we are given the grace not just to see His power from a distance, but to become overwhelmed and caught up by it firsthand. And this has been how God has always done it. God's people are not just uninvolved bystanders. Throughout all of history, it has always been through weak, unable, and ill-equipped men and women. Abraham and Sarah were too old to have children, but God worked through them to build a people too numerous to count. Moses lacked eloquence and had a massive anger problem, but God used him to lead a nation out of captivity and sustain them in the wilderness for 40 years. And when he received opposition, it wasn't by his power, it was by God's power that those people were dealt with appropriately. When it was Gideon from the weakest of all the clans and he only had 300 men to fight, God used this feeble man in his forces to deliver Israel out of the hand of Midian.

David was overlooked and the least of many strong brothers. Many. But God made him king and defeated Israel's enemies. Jeremiah was young, yet God used him to preach to a people during the fall of Jerusalem and to preach a promise of a new covenant. Amos was a forgettable farm hand, but God chose him to speak of true justice and righteousness. Jonah was a jerk and didn't like the people he was sent to serve, but God used him to bring about radical repentance in the absolute least place anybody would expect it. All of the disciples that Jesus called were foolish and no matter how close they stood to Jesus, they almost always missed what he was doing. Almost always. Yet God would use them to take the Gospel to the corners of the earth. Then there's you and I.

The Bible says our history is that we were all dead in our trespasses and sins. We were so drunk with our own desire and polluted by the world that we never even had a chance. But God, being rich in mercy, made us alive together with Christ. And He has raised us with Christ and seated us with Him above. This is where our math needs to be corrected, brothers and sisters. That is the way that we count success. If we aim to be a people who want to see the power of God in ministry and our lives, we need to be transformed by it and to have our hair singed by His displays of might and our minds blown out by His providence. And to do this, we must be a people that run towards dependence. We have to know that we are all just vessels filled with the truth of who He is and what He's done.

That is the sum of who we are. All that we are is entirely wrapped up in His presence in our lives. Success for those who follow God, for Christians, is the life dependent on Jesus. While Paul tells us that success is not counted the same as the world, he also shows us that it's not measured in the same way either. Paul speaks of the standard of measurement of success being found in Jesus alone when he says, "We are always carrying in the body of Jesus, the death of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus may also be manifested in our bodies, for we who live are always being given over to death for Jesus' sake. So that the life of Jesus also may be manifest in our mortal flesh. So death is at work in us, but life in you." The standard is Jesus. Because Jesus, though as the Son of God, He did not know dependence or weakness, but He chose to empty Himself by becoming a normal man and walking in weakness.

To walk in weakness. By going through being born, by learning how to walk, by living through all the trials and the temptations we all know, He did all of it and He remained completely dependent on God. No matter if it was a long day of healing and helping people or in the garden, right before His death, He was there, praying and living out His dependence, giving His weakness over, and doing everything following in faithfulness. The Bible tells us clearly that Jesus endured suffering and shame because of the joy that was set before Him. In His being normal, although He felt everything, His dependence revealed the power of God that would outdo whatever suffering He needed to endure. Even unjust accusation and horrible death would be subdued by God's might. And as He lives, even now, and is seated at the right hand, His weakness, the uncreated stepping into creation, was to show the power and the might of the triune God and His mission to rescue all of creation.

So when Paul says, I carry the dying of Christ in my body so that the life would be seen, he speaks of being measured according to the true standard of weakness. Being caught up in the manifest power of God, Jesus in His life, death, and resurrection. When measured and counted appropriately, the weakness we despise, the dependence we try to avoid, becomes a light socket directly connected to the nuclear reactor. We have incalculable access to an unmeasured power in our weakness, in our being opposed by the world. It is not unreasonable to see and to feel the weight of difficulty in life. It's normal to see trials and tribulations as hard and discouraging. You're lying to yourself if you don't think that's the case. The grace in these, the gift we have, is that not only are we not overcome by them, but we serve a mighty Lord who loves to show us who He is by whipping those trials and tribulations into incredible demonstrations of His power.

So when the call comes, we don't have to feel bad if we see our inability, but we can be certainly comforted in the power we are about to see Him demonstrate. When serving others for Jesus gets difficult and you realize you're not getting everywhere, even feeling opposed, it's okay to feel your inadequacy. You are inadequate. But no, your Lord will not be slowed down to accomplish His purposes. When you are called to a task and all you can see is the long list of reasons why you cannot do it, anticipate a good and loving Lord who will teach you and show you an even longer list of glories to see and experience. Brothers and sisters, success for the Christian is the life dependent on Jesus. Although many of us feel our weakness this morning and need to be encouraged to look and to see the power of God in our lives, the way too often I don't actually find myself in that position.

If I'm honest, the path to get there, the path to just recognize my weakness, the path to dependence is a long and arduous journey to find how to do it myself. It's only after failing to find that, that I've become dependent. Because I try to put my own power and might on display. This is what Paul was addressing in the Corinthian church. They wanted Paul to flex. They were embarrassed that he wouldn't and embarrassed that they were associated with him because he wouldn't. If you read both letters we have to the Corinthians, you'll find that what Paul is most often addressing is the basic air of self-importance. I think this is most of our weakness. It is the repetition of being sold a message that we can do it. That we're special.

That we came out of the womb with some kind of advantage. That we've earned everything we have and owe nothing to anyone. Our weakness is that we are proud of our independence. If you're prone to this like me, if you feel this creeping up in your heart at all, see it as it is. A weakness. It may be a strength in a hundred different ways, but it will never compare to the hand of God in history. Paul instructs the Colossians to, "Just as you received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in Him." Or live in Him. We don't just receive salvation by grace, but we live by it too. Don't lose yourself in the effort of trying to muscle your way through the Christian life. As a Christian life starts by being dependent on who Jesus is and what He has done, so also a Christian life matures by remaining dependent on who Jesus is and what He has done.

As Paul reminds us, our confidence is in Jesus and His resurrection. That confidence creates dependence, and our dependence always binds power.

Brothers and sisters, I hate to inform you, but we are not the little engines that could. We don't need to dig deep, or get in the right mindset. Not because of all the difficulties that come are too strong, but because the truth is, we never had the power to begin with. We are like a powerless train car that goes nowhere unless it's hitched to Christ. He is our power and He carries us through every obstacle and every difficulty. We have no need to find extra fuel. No reason to compare ourselves with any others. And no matter the weather or visibility of what comes ahead, we can trust that we will make it where we are headed. So instead of telling ourselves that we can or we think we can, let us look to Him, depend on Him, and watch in amazement as His glory and power are put on display in our weakness.

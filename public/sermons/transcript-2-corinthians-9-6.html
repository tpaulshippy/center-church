2 Corinthians 9:6

Rich Richardson · Feb 16, 2025

Generous People are Joyful People

The Bible gives us all kinds of examples, people who are moved toward radical and generous living. Examples of people who are radically and joyfully generous.

We go to the Bible, if you're new with us and you're wondering why we preach from this book, it's because we believe that it says, and it's just a conviction of ours, that the Bible is living. It's active. It's able to accomplish the purposes of God as it's preached. So that's the conviction we're heading into with this morning as we go to the Bible. But looking back to the Old Testament, one example of radical and joyful generosity is that of Boaz, who despite the risk of financial ruin and reputational risks, let Ruth work his field. It turned out to be a massive blessing to Ruth for her and her mother-in-law. And eventually, Boaz winds up redeeming Naomi and her Moabite daughter-in-law, which was a radical act of generosity. Another example of this sort of generosity is found in 1 Kings 10. After hearing about the fame of an Israelite king, the Queen of Sheba travels to Jerusalem because she hears about this guy that possessed all the wisdom in the world.

She wanted to make sure that this was true. But once she finds out that Solomon was really who people said he was, she just dumps a ton of gifts all over him. The Bible tells us she gave the king 120 talents of gold and a very great quantity of spices and precious stones. Now, the reason she did this is because she recognized something different about this king compared to the other kings of the ancient Near East. She actually tells Solomon, Blessed be the Lord your God, who has delighted in you and set you on the throne of Israel.

I think that being in the presence of God's hand-picked king does something to people.

The New Testament is then filled with even more examples of generous people. Think about the wise men who traveled from distances far to visit Mary and Joseph after baby Jesus was born. And they bring with them, the Bible tells us, gifts, gold and frankincense and myrrh. Again, when people are in the presence of a king, something happens. There's a transformative effect, and they become radically and joyfully generous. Then there's Zacchaeus, the one the children's song tells us is a wee little man from Luke 19. He just wanted to get a glimpse of Jesus. It's all he wanted. But he gets even more than that. Luke tells us that Zacchaeus received Jesus into his home joyfully.

And Zacchaeus, we're told, becomes saved. As a result of this salvation and joy, he sells half of what he owns and gives it to the poor.

You see, there are all kinds of examples of radical, joyful generosity in the scriptures. And that's because something happens to people when they meet with King Jesus. Something incredible happens.

Not only are eternal trajectories transformed, that they are, but even more, something happens in our hearts, in the here and now. Something miraculous takes place. So our task today is to go to 2 Corinthians 9, which shows us what actually happens in the here and now. When you meet with King Jesus, we'll see what incredible theological truth transforms the way that we live, the way that we serve others.

But here's the thing.

We live in a fallen world. We live in a world that's opposed to the powerful effects of the gospel. And because of sin, because of external sin, because of the sin that resides in our hearts, there are several temptations that we must be aware of. Temptations that we must battle against when it comes to our own radical and joyful generosity.

This is not an exhaustive list, but here are a few. One temptation of ours is to prioritize wealth and abundance. I believe that's the biggest temptation that faces us in the West.

We fixate on retirement funds.

We hedge off wealth for posterity. And we do this for the sake of easy living, of comfort.

These things are symptomatic of a deeper desire. Desire for autonomy.

Desire for self-sufficiency. Desire for isolationism. This self-centered way of living, it cordons us off. It cuts us off from gospel-centered community. Because if we spend every waking moment, every hour consumed with how we'll accumulate more, we'll miss out on how we can truly live a radically and joyfully gospel-centered life.

Another temptation is to view a passage like 2 Corinthians 9 as formulaic, as a way to get more from God. A la the prosperity gospel. Rich mentioned that last week. Now the thing about the prosperity gospel is it's a contradiction of the true gospel. Because what it does is it promises material blessing and that is then linked to how much you give. It's linked. There's this formula that we can be tricked into thinking that if only we give more, we will get more. All for the sake of self-centered living.

Yet another temptation is for us to minimize or downplay our own ability to be radically and joyfully generous.

You might think you don't own enough to be generous.

You might think that you're not in a season of life right now for you to be generous. and on the flip side of that there may be some in this room who are tempted to think well I'm already pretty generous. This message is for other people not for me.

Friends, I don't want you to miss out on what God has for you in this passage. There is much that we can be challenged with. There is much that can cause us to grow in being radically and joyfully generous.

We need to see that generosity is rooted in the gospel. It's an outworking of gospel-centered living. It's rooted in the very character of who our God is.

God is generous with his gifts and he's even more generous with his grace.

Paul in just that last chapter that we went through chapter 8 told us that for our sake Jesus became poor.

Friends, there's no better depiction of radical and joyful generosity than that.

And so it should be our collective goal together this morning and then all the days ahead to grow in our generosity to see how we can be challenged to grow to become radically and joyfully generous.

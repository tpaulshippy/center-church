Acts 14:21-28

Rich Richardson · Acts · Aug 16, 2026

The Story of Acts

Thanks, Joelle. Yeah, we'll be in the last couple paragraphs of Acts 14. So go ahead and turn in your Bibles there, or on your devices, if that's what you prefer.

As I pause and ask for God's help this morning, Lord, you desire to care for us this morning. In our weaknesses and in our sufferings. You desire to fortify us with your word and spur us on to love and good deeds.

And you desire to do so right here, right now, in our midst, in this local church, through the proclamation of your word.

Let your kingdom come.

Let your will be done. And let us grow in our resolve to follow hard after you through the power of your Holy Spirit. In Jesus' name, amen.

Now, if you've been with us the last few Sundays, the book of Acts has taken us on a breathtaking adventure. You could call it Mr. Paul and Barnabas' wild ride.

In chapter 13, we saw Jewish magicians go blind and a Roman proconsul grow astonished at the teaching of the Lord. In chapter 14, we watched crippled men stand up to walk. And crowds bow down to worship the apostles as if they were pagan gods. And at times, along the way, bystanders have begged them, the apostles, to just stick around. And at other times, the same people have chased them out of town.

In fact, at their latest stop, in a town called Lystra, the fickle crowds went so far as to throw stones at Paul, drag him outside the city, and leave him for dead.

Not once have Barnabas and Paul had time to stay in any one place for long.

Not once have they been able to see a community of believers take root. They keep getting kicked out.

Not once has the relentless persecution against them receded enough to allow them to establish a local church.

That all changes with today's text. Care for and formation of disciples and church planning finally takes center stage. Watch for it as we read our text in Acts 14, beginning in verse 19. And I'll be reading from the English Standard Version.

Now in Lystra, Jews came from Antioch and Iconium. And having persuaded the crowds, they stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city, supposing that he was dead.

But when the disciples gathered around him, he rose up and entered the city. And on the next day, he went on with Barnabas to Derbe.

And when they had preached the gospel to that city, Derbe, and had made many disciples, they returned to Lystra and to Iconium and to Antioch, strengthening the souls of those disciples, encouraging them to continue in the faith and saying that through many tribulations, we must enter the kingdom of God.

And when they had appointed elders for them in every church with prayer and fasting, they committed them to the Lord in whom they had believed.

Then they passed through Pisidia and came to Pamphylia. And when they had spoken the word in Perga, they went down to Atalia. And from there they sailed to Antioch where they had been commended to the grace of God for the work that they had fulfilled. And when they arrived and gathered the church together, they declared all that God had done with them and how he had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles.

And they remained no little time with the disciples.

Imagine a bloodied, bruised, and battered Paul limping the 80 miles on foot from the outskirts of Lystra, where he had been stoned, to the town of Derby.

And for the first time in a long while, he's not persecuted.

And he's given some time to convalesce.

At last, he and Barnabas can stay in one place long enough to get down to the serious business of making disciples.

And as verse 21 of our text tells us, that's exactly what they did. Many disciples were made in Derby.

And the text also implies that enough time elapsed for those disciples in Derby to be strengthened and encouraged to continue in the faith. Verse 22. And that elders were appointed among them and that they were committed to the Lord in verse 23.

At last, Paul and Barnabas had succeeded in the work of God that they'd been called to do. They had planted their first church.

But then, something unexpected happened. Or at least, from the perspective of the church in Derby, something unexpected happened.

Just when everything seemed to be going so well, their favorite founding apostles decided to up and leave them.

And they weren't leaving to find a safe haven to relax, to recharge, or to catch some me time. No, they were planning to return to the danger zone, to the very place where they had last been chased from town, pummeled with rocks, and left for dead. Paul and Barnabas set their faces and were determined to go back to Lystra.

But why? Were they gluttons for punishment?

Did they just want to pick a fight? The citizens of Lystra and nearby Oconium and Antioch wanted them dead. Returning to that territory was fraught with risk. It wasn't safe.

Why go, Paul? Stay with us. Don't you see that the fields are white for harvest here in Derby?

Our church has success written all over it. Besides, can anything good come out of that tiny, insignificant town called Lystra?

And Paul's response, stop breaking my heart. There are disciples at Lystra. Disciples who gathered around me as I lay writhing on the ground in a puddle of blood.

That's what it says in verse 20. The disciples gathered around him. Disciples who nursed me in that moment, treating my wounds, bandaging my injuries, and attending to my needs. Those disciples need help. They desire to follow Jesus, but they don't know how. They lack wisdom and knowledge and discernment. They have no experienced role models to emulate, and they are trying to follow Jesus in a very, very hostile environment.

Their neighbors have rejected them. Their friends have abandoned them. Their families have disowned them. They are alone. We can't leave them there. We have to go back. And strengthen what remains.

For it is to this that our Lord Jesus has commissioned us.

Remember what he said when he was still with us?

Go, therefore, and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I commanded you.

Second to the last verse in Matthew.

Where there are disciples to be made, baptized, and taught, we must go.

So, to Lystra, they returned.

And what did they do when they arrived?

Verse 22 tells us they strengthened the souls of those disciples.

Those same disciples who had physically nursed Paul as he lay lifeless on the ground needed a spiritual nursing themselves. Indeed, they needed a spiritual resuscitation after so many months of isolation.

But exactly how is that to be done? How does one get strengthened spiritually?

Well, in the physical realm, we strengthen our bodies by eating food.

And there is only one source of food for the soul.

It's the word of God.

What does Jeremiah say?

Your words were found and I ate them. Your words became for me a joy.

And even Jesus said something like that when he was tempted in the wilderness, quoting the law, man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God.

So for the disciples of Lystra, Paul got busy feeding them with God's word. He preached it, he taught it, he instructed them in it day and night. And little by little their souls were strengthened. They started to grow in their walk with God like toddlers. They went from crawling on their bellies to scooting on their knees to standing up and taking those first steps of faith. And all because they were steadfastly fed the life-giving, soul-strengthening word of God. But there's more. Not only were their souls strengthened, they were also, as verse 22 says, encouraged to continue in the faith.

Here's where our brother Barnabas comes into play. Where Paul's specialty was delivery of the word, Barnabas was all about bringing the encouragement.

Remember what his name means? Son of encouragement.

So as the congregation at Lister began to walk on their own two feet, Barnabas encouraged them every step of the way. But how he encouraged them may surprise you.

Notice what it says there in the latter half of verse 22. They were encouraged with this saying.

Through many tribulations, we must enter the kingdom of God.

Now at first read, that doesn't sound very encouraging. I can think of better ways to entice churchgoers to keep up their attendance than by reminding them that what awaits them is a road full of ditches, potholes, toils, and trouble. Who wants to sign up for that?

But look at who's talking here. Paul and Barnabas have known more than their fair share of tribulation to date.

Their path has been littered with insults, hardships, persecutions, and calamities all along the way. And as noted earlier, the believers at Lystra have seen no shortage of trouble either, isolated from their community.

They've all endured many tribulations, and they will experience many more in the days to come.

So if it was true for them, is it true for us?

Must we go through many tribulations Christians also?

The short answer to that question is yes.

Think about it. we live in a fallen world. Trouble is all around us.

It's unavoidable, whether you are a believer or not. And the longer you go through life, the more trouble you'll find. And the more trouble will find you and devour you, and the longer it will last.

Some of us will lose our jobs and stay unemployed for a very long time.

Some of us will lose our wealth and remain impoverished for years to come.

Some will lose close friendships, never to see them restored or replaced.

Some know, actually most of us will eventually lose our health and have it steadily decline until we die. Yes, all of us go through many tribulations in this life, but not everyone who endures that kind of trouble will enter the kingdom of God.

Let's revisit and reread verse 22 again. through many tribulations, we must enter the kingdom of God. It's not the first part of that statement that is encouraging. It's the final part. Do you see it there? those who continue in the faith must enter the kingdom of God. Do you hear what's being communicated? This statement is more a promise than a command. Those who continue in the faith must enter the kingdom of God. it's a predetermined outcome. Everyone who continues in the faith must enter God's kingdom because God himself has guaranteed that we will. that's God's promise to everyone who believes and continue in the faith.

Certified bona fide entry into the kingdom of God.

And God guarantees that entry no matter how many tribulations we encounter and no matter how many tribulations we go through along the way.

So yes the disciples at Lystra were encouraged by this word for through their many tribulations they were well on their way to entering the kingdom of God.

And thankfully they were not doing it alone. In the short time that Paul and Barnabas were there with them the Lord faithfully created a new community from a hodgepodge of strangers who had avoided one another in the past initially this group had very little in common.

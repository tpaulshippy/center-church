Acts 5:12-42

Rich Richardson · Acts · Apr 12, 2026

Fear God not man, come what may.

We know, as Christians, we follow a crucified Savior. Yes, He was triumphantly brought back to life from the realm of the dead to an indestructible life, but the fact remains, He was horribly mistreated in His life for no good reason.

That also means that His followers often will be mistreated for the sole purpose of just following Jesus. This is a promise we get directly from Jesus Himself. On the night He was betrayed, He said this, If the world hates you, and by the world He doesn't mean the earth, He doesn't mean the plants and the animals, He means the people. If the world hates you, know that it hated me before it hated you. If you were of the world, the world would love you as its own. But because you are not of the world, but I chose you out of the world, therefore the world hates you. You see, there will be people who hate Christians just because they follow Jesus. And this happens even in pluralistic United States of America. Christians used to be regarded in our culture as strange and quirky kind of people.

Now, because of our belief that Jesus is the only way to eternal life, that exclusivity now is seen by our culture as threatening, ominous, and sinister.

But this is nothing new.

Animosity against the followers of Jesus date back to our earliest brothers and sisters. In fact, gathered in the cloud of witnesses cheering us on right now, our brothers and sisters who've gone before us, who testified to Jesus Christ even when they knew it would cost them their lives. As we look at Acts chapter 5, we see the more things change, the more they stay exactly the same. And as we watch these troubles come upon the saints in Acts chapter 5, we must ask ourselves this. If following Jesus is an invitation to suffer, how can we remain steadfast? If following Jesus is an invitation to suffer, how can we remain steadfast? When voices in our world threaten and terrorize, how do we stand strong?

By fearing the Lord.

The fear of the Lord really is the beginning of wisdom. See, when we fear the Lord, we regard Him as the most pressing reality in the universe. When we stand, we stand firm on the blood of Jesus Christ and the rock that He is, and as we stand, we're called to stand to honor Him above all else, even though we may court trouble. That's exactly what happens in Acts chapter 5. Acts 5 is not just a story for us to read and learn. No, this is a call. A call to us that says this. Fear God. Not man, come what may.

Advent 2025: Nicene Creed - Eternal Son, For Us As Us

Rich Richardson · Dec 7, 2025 · 00:48:24

Christ became what we are to save us from who we were.

As you've noticed, we began our Advent series last Sunday, and because this is the 1700th year of the meeting at Nicaea, we started working through the Nicene Creed together. During this series, what we're doing is we're focusing on the section of the Creed that details who Jesus is, and today we arrive at one of the most beautiful statements in that Creed. For us and for our salvation, he came down from heaven. This phrase, actually, it summarizes the reason for why we celebrate Advent. And while this is a remarkable statement, there is another truth that lies behind it. There's something that this statement implies, something that's easy to forget if you've been walking with Jesus for a while. This truth assumes something about you and something about me. We all needed saving. Before walking with Jesus, we weren't good people who needed just a little bit of encouragement and a pat on the back.

We weren't misguided people who needed a course redirection, who needed advice or even a life coach to make our lives better. We were all slaves. We were bound, helpless, and spiritually imprisoned.

Maybe you've been a Christian for a few months, maybe a few years, perhaps even decades.

Maybe you grew up in the church and you can't even remember a time where you didn't believe in God. It's easy to forget who we once were apart from Christ. It's easy to develop a spiritual amnesia and forget the spiritual condition from which Jesus has rescued us. It's easy to forget that every single one of us, without exception, were born into a prison we could not escape from if not for grace.

So what we have in Galatians chapter 4 is Paul reminding his readers and us this morning of our miraculous rescue. In one sentence, here's the hope that's found in this passage, and I believe in the Nicene Creed.

That is, Christ became what we are to save us from who we were. Christ became what we are to save us from who we were.

You see, the incarnation, it's the heartbeat of the gospel. The eternal son willingly stepped into history not to observe our plight from a distance. No. In love, he came to step into our existence by taking on our humanity, bearing our curse, and making us his own.

So let's read about this great hope that we have, beginning in Galatians 4.3. Friends, this is God's word for us this morning.

In the same way, we also, when we were children, were enslaved to the elementary principles of the world. But when the fullness of time had come, God sent forth his Son, born of woman, born under the law, to redeem those who were under the law, so that we might receive adoption as sons.

And because you're sons, God has sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, crying, Abba, Father.

So you are no longer a slave, but a son. And if a son, then an heir through God.

Our first point this morning is who we were. Before Christ rescued us, before any of us were Christians, we were enslaved. That's who we were. Paul tells us what we were enslaved to in verse 3 when he writes, We were enslaved to the elementary principles of the world. Now, commentators interpret this phrase with a lot of nuance. It's very different across the theological spectrum. But Paul is speaking about the fundamental spiritual powers and systems that held both Jews and Gentiles into spiritual bondage. In Galatians chapter 3, verse 23, Paul says that Israel was held captive under the law. Not because the law was evil. No, their hearts were evil.

Romans chapter 7, verse 12, reads that the law is holy and the commandment is holy, righteous, and good. So Jews, they couldn't blame the law for their plight into slavery.

It was sin that warped the human heart and distorted our view of everything so that God's good law became a prison.

The law revealed sin, but it could not rescue us from sin. It could expose guilt, but it couldn't expiate or remove our guilt.

Thus, the Jew was a slave, living under commandments he could not keep, weighed down by a system that he couldn't fulfill.

In Galatians 4, chapter 4, verse 8, Paul says, This enslavement speaks to how Gentiles worshipped pagan deities, which were gods of the material world. These were gods that were developed based upon what they could sense.

So the material world around them, they built statues and idols to worship these pagan deities. As a result, their lives were governed by fear, by superstition, manipulation, and self-righteous attempts to appease these gods.

So what we have are two different forms of bondage.

Bondage to the law and bondage to the elementary principles, the spirits of the world.

Both reveal the same spiritual condition. Enslavement. And before our conversion, we were no different. Maybe you weren't enslaved to Jewish ceremonial law. Maybe you didn't bow down to pagan idols. But that doesn't mean you weren't a slave. Some of us were enslaved to prosperity. Our hearts were controlled. That every beat of our heart was controlled by the desire to accumulate more, loving money more than we love God.

Some of us were enslaved to fear. Living our lives as though worst-case scenarios are sovereign over us.

Some of us were enslaved to our comfort, making every decision where to send our kids to school, where to move, which job to take, based on what feels safe or comfortable.

Not on what glorifies God.

Some of us were enslaved to apathy. We were indifferent to everyone and everything around us.

Some of us were enslaved to lust, body image, achievement, approval, relationships, control, success. The list goes on. Now, these chains may look different. But friends, they're still chains.

Now, lest we think we're so far removed in our modern day from the historical situation of the Galatians, we have to realize one thing. The Galatian problem is our problem.

The churches in Galatia were being influenced by these teachers, false teachers. They were called the Judaizers.

The Judaizers said, Jesus, yeah, he's important. But he's not enough.

What they did is they preached a Jesus plus gospel. Jesus plus circumcision. Jesus plus observance of festivals. Jesus plus works of the law.

So what Paul says in this letter is that's slavery.

Anytime you say Jesus is not enough, you're putting yourself back under bondage.

Now, we don't face Judaizers today, but the lies that they told are alive and well. Because we could be tempted to believe the lie of, I need Jesus plus morality. Or, I need Jesus plus religious performance. I need Jesus plus a mystical spiritual experience. I need Jesus plus the approval of people.

The gospel is predicated upon the person and work of Jesus Christ. Not your work.

Nothing you do or anything anyone else can do can help you.

And this should provide us with loads of encouragement.

Because we don't need to perform for people to earn our status or to have a reshaped identity.

If you place upon yourself a desire to look a certain way for people, to present some sort of self-image of you or your family to others that is just not realistic, friend, let me tell you, you struggle with people-pleasing in fear of man.

But the gospel frees us from the inner desire to please people. And it causes us to want to please the Lord.

We simply walk in the freedom that Christ has secured for us.

Christ came to free us. Christ became what we are so he could save us from who we were. For those of you who haven't been set free yet, those of you who don't believe in Jesus or think you believe in Jesus, you need to be set free.

You need freedom. Maybe you have some familiarity with Jesus, who he is, but you have no desire to serve him.

Consider your spiritual condition.

You are enslaved to the elementary principles of the world.

You are bound and captive to your desires. Your cravings, your compulsions. You need to be redeemed from your sin and set free from your captivity.

But for those of us in Christ, that's who we once were.

We were slaves, powerless to free ourselves, bound under sin, whether it was through law-keeping or idol worship.

When we consider who we were, it helps us treasure who Christ is.

Praise God that Paul doesn't end the letter there at verse 3 because he doesn't stop because the gospel never leaves us just staring at old chains.

Instead, Paul lifts our eyes to see the one who became what we are so he could save us from who we were. Second point, who Jesus is.

The eternal God was sent by God to become fully human to redeem fully enslaved sinners.

What Paul does in these two verses is he gives us three descriptions of how this happens. Three windows into the mystery of the incarnation that we can now peer through. First, he tells us the timing of the incarnation. when he writes when the fullness of time had come. Second, Paul states the manner by which Jesus came. He was born under a woman. Third, he describes the mission Jesus came to accomplish. He was born under the law. Describing how Jesus came to obey where we failed.

The first window that we gaze through is how Paul says he came in the fullness of time.

The fullness of time means the exact moment God appointed for redemptive history to reach its climactic high point.

Historians often point out various features in the ancient world that made it an effective time for the gospel to spread. First one is the Pax Romana. Caesar Augustus inaugurated the Pax Romana or the Roman Peace and the Roman Empire at that time experienced a period of relative peace from any war or conflict. It was a time that allowed for unrestricted travel in the Empire which did lead to the free flow of ideas throughout the Empire.

Another hypothesis for what sparked this fullness of time was there was a common trade language that was spoken throughout the known world at that time which was called Koine Greek. Koine was the lingua franca of that time. It was like English is today spoken throughout the world throughout the known world and because there was a common language ideas could be communicated and understood by those who spoke a different secondary language or primary language. Another feature of the fullness of time were the unified travel routes that existed throughout the Empire. this infrastructure led to again the free flow of ideas.

Now you could say these observations are helpful but they're merely tangential to the main point. So what is the main point behind the fullness of time?

Paul's saying that God's timing was perfect.

God and his providential sovereign knowledge knew exactly when the incarnation had to take place.

Thomas Aquinas once reasoned it something like this. If the incarnation came too early our pride would not have been humbled.

Meaning we would not have realized the great treasure that the incarnation is if Christ had come too early.

If the incarnation came too late Aquinas reasoned hope would have turned to despair.

If Christ delayed in his first coming it would have left people hopeless.

Instead the Son of God came precisely when God the Father intended. The moment history reached its divinely ordained turning point. All of history prior to the incarnation was headed toward this singular event. And now all of history is headed toward the second advent or the second coming of our Lord.

There are many implications to this. Many. But the greatest of all is this.

God knows exactly what you need and when you need it.

He knows better than you do when to act in blessing. He knows better than you do when to remain silent. The one thing we can be assured of about God's holy nature is his timing is always perfect.

The second window we peer through in verse four is he didn't merely appear to be human as some of the heretics believed in the early church the main heresy being docetism. He didn't disguise himself as human. He became fully and authentically human. He assumed both human body and soul without there being any change or confusion to his divine nature as the son of God.

Think about this.

The one who designed the laws of thermodynamics of gravity of motion he entered a world that was bound by those laws. The one who upholds the universe by the word of his power was held in the arms of a teenage mother. The one who never tires took on flesh that grows weary. The immortal God of all ages took on our mortality.

When Jesus nursed at Mary's breast he was simultaneously sustaining all of creation including his mother.

This is the breathtaking mystery at the center of our faith. Christ the Lord of glory became what we are so he could save us from who we were. This is why the Nicene Creed says he came down from heaven was incarnate and became man.

I realize what I'm about to say is going to be polemical but I'm going to say it anyways. This matters because there are still cults that believe in various heresies like the Jehovah's witnesses who deny that Jesus is the God man. It also matters because of where we live. We are surrounded by those who call themselves latter-day saints.

The LDS are not Christians precisely because they don't adhere to a fundamentally Christian truth.

Jesus Christ always existed with God as God. But in the incarnation he assumed a human nature. He didn't need to deify a human body as the LDS believe. From the moment of his conception he was fully God and fully man.

It's important for us to understand that Jesus was born of a woman because we must believe that he assumed a human nature. He was both body and soul.

It's important because he would not be for us if he weren't one of us.

He could not save us and defeat death if he didn't undergo suffering and taste of death himself. And he'd be unable to save if he were only God.

The third thing Paul says about the incarnation is that he was born under the law.

This conveys that Jesus was truly obedient. He didn't just become some random human on the continent of Africa.

He became a Jewish man who lived under the full demands of the Mosaic law. He did this because this is how God chose to reveal himself to his people in the Old Testament.

And so Jesus lived under the full demands of every commandment, every requirement, every obligation of the law. And he fulfilled each perfectly. And so and so what we have in Jesus is where Adam failed, Christ succeeded. Where Israel sinned, Christ obeyed. Where we stumble and give into our temptations, Christ never does, never did in his earthly ministry. He became human to obey in our place. Friend, this obedience, it is the foundation of the assurance that we have as Christians. He lived our life so we could receive his righteousness.

If you struggle with some besetting sin, this means you can run to the Savior for help. And he will help you. He doesn't demand perfection from us. So when we do fail to meet his standard of holiness, which we will, will continually to fail this side of eternity, we have one who will help us.

He knows what it's like to be tempted in the ways we're tempted yet never caved in to temptation. He never gave in.

And it was this obedience all the way to his death, marching up the hill of Golgotha with that cross that has secured Paula righteousness.

And so friend, he doesn't look on you with disdain when you make a mess of things.

He loves you. He robes you with his righteousness. He loves us enough to have taken upon himself all our sin. Because the reality is he not only obeyed the law in our place, yes he did, but he also bore the curse of the law in our place.

Because perfect obedience was never enough.

Guilt had to be removed. And so the one who fulfilled all the demands of the law, he took upon himself the weight of our sin at the cross.

Christ didn't take on flesh to just show us what God is like, although he did. He also took on flesh to accomplish something for you and for me.

So what Paul does in verses 5 through 7 is he shows us what all of Christ coming and obeying achieved. Point number three, who we are in Christ.

Because Christ became one of us, we can become children of God.

Paul tells us in Galatians 4, that as a result of the incarnation and of Jesus' mediatorial work, we are adopted as sons and daughters into God's family.

Because redemption is not merely God forgiving sinners.

Something more happens. God brings sinners into his family. That's what's meant by the word adoption.

Now Paul uses that word adoption because it refers to something that the Galatians in their time would have understood immediately. The Roman household.

Family was so important in Roman society that if a family was without an heir, adoption was a legal step that the head of the family could take so that the family would continue on and not become extinct.

So a child could be adopted and the arrangement would be mutually beneficial. That poor family would receive material compensation and their child would be afforded with great privilege.

The rich family or the pater familias as it was called would receive an heir.

Think about that for a moment.

Think place your shoes in this adopted child's shoes. Everything would be different.

Just like that, everything would be different. The price the adoptee would have to pay is completely breaking away from their old family.

But the benefit they would receive is sonship into a family of great privilege. Paul uses skillfully this adoption language to summarize what happens when a believer receives their new identity in Christ. It's skillful because it conveys that we've received a new identity. We no longer belong to our old slave masters. We have a new master and he is good.

It conveys that we now have a new name. If you're united to Christ by faith, you bear his name. You're not a drunkard any longer or a liar, a cheater, an idolater, a pornographer, a womanizer. You're a Christian. And as a Christian, you're a son and a daughter of God.

You have a new home. friends, we're so we're sojourners here. We are pilgrims in this land that we live in. This, my friends, is Babylon.

We're not meant to set up shop and get comfortable in this house because God has prepared a better one for us.

And we receive a new inheritance. friends, God doesn't save us halfway. He doesn't pull us out of the prison of our sin to place us in a halfway house. He welcomes us into his own house. And we aren't just guests in his house.

Do you ever have the feeling when you go into someone's home and they say, make yourself at home? I always find myself thinking, that's impossible. I can't do that.

I mean, I can try. I'll try hard, but I just can't. Now you guys know if you invite me into your home, what I'll be thinking if you ask me that question.

It's not like that in God's house.

We have full privilege, full access. In his house, what is his, is truly ours.

We aren't servants in his house. We're not his employees, we're his children. And as children, we're heirs. Heirs of what?

We get God himself. That's what we receive. Because God is the gospel.

Now this has many, many implications for us. The biggest one being that we now receive his spirit. Paul says in verse six, because your sons, God sent the spirit of his son into our hearts crying, Abba, Father.

Not only do we gain this new status, this new identity, we receive his spirit.

We gain a new heart, a new heartbeat. The spirit himself lives within us, awakening new affections for him. His spirit assures us of God's love. If you're ever doubting that God's love is for you, the spirit of God will remind you that you belong to him.

The spirit teaches us to speak to him like children speaking to a father.

Some of us live as though God the father is cold and distant to us.

Or maybe you think that he's hesitant to love you. But Paul says the spirit is crying out within you. He is your father. You belong to him.

This radically changes the way we approach God.

It changes the way we approach him because now we can approach him with vulnerability because he knows how weak we are. He knows our weaknesses yet he still gives us his spirit. Why wouldn't we want to go to him when we're feeling down? Why would we go to lesser goods when we can go to the highest good?

It also changes how frequently we approach him.

We should be as eager to talk to our father in heaven as we are with those closest to us.

We should run to God first thing in the morning with all of our cares and concerns before we dump them on our spouse.

Why?

Because he loves you more than your spouse does.

He knows better than your spouse how to help you. the God radically changes everything about us and the presence of his spirit is proof of our adoption.

When we have the spirit of God dwelling in us he gives us this experiential knowledge of God. It's not abstract. It doesn't exist on some server somewhere. It's in your heart. You can experience the power of God. This means we're not just passive in the Christian life. We are endowed with great access to God and unimpeded communion with him.

What this means is you'll be more enamored with the glories of Christ than anything else. You'll be more excited to come to church on a Sunday than if you had received front row seats to the Super Bowl.

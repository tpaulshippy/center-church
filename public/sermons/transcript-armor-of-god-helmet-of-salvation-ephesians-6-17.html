Armor of God: Helmet of Salvation (Ephesians 6:17)

Rich Richardson · Feb 26, 2023

The vision of our helmet of salvation in Ephesians 6:17 gives an unfettered confidence. Together we remind ourselves that vision for what we have in Jesus is more powerful than the sight we have of our troubles. Our assured salvation should stir up abounding confidence in Jesus.

Henry Wanyoki is, was a Kenyan long-distance runner. He was so gifted as a teenager that his times in the 5,000 and 10,000 meters approached world class in high school. Before graduating high school, everybody in the Kenyan running circles knew that this was going to be another in the long line of long-distance Kenyan titans, but alas, it was not to be. On May 1st, 1995, Wanyoki suffered a stroke as he slept and it robbed his sight. He said, "I went to bed, a normal person, the following day I awoke and found myself in darkness." From there he tumbled into an oppressive despair. After years of trouble and hardship and discouragement and depression, someone suggested that he take up running again, this time with the assistance of a guide. So he did. At first, he didn't know that there was such a thing as running when you're blind.

At first he was afraid and he was constantly unsure of his next step. He would step and fall and slip, but his guide, tethered to him, would guide him along. After a while, a problem arose. Henry got so good again that they couldn't find a guide fast enough who would be able to keep up with him. Henry became a world-class, blind, distance runner and broke records in the 2000 Sydney Paralympics. Michelle Hamilton wrote a story on Henry in the runner's world a few years ago. She described something, she said something Henry said that jumped out at me. Henry said, "Vision is more powerful than sight." Let me say that again, "Vision is more powerful than sight."

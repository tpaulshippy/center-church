Good Friday 2025

Rich Richardson · Apr 19, 2025 · 00:32:50

God Forsake Jesus so That we Would Never be Forsaken

Established in the 17th century, the workhouses of Great Britain were a place where poor men, women, and children lived, worked, and went to school. Workhouses were contrived by the British government as a place where the poor could redeem themselves. This was hardly ever the case.

Workhouses were notorious for their harsh living conditions, the strict rules. They had a reputation for being known as a place of last resort for the poor. Tales of the Workhouse, they cast a dark shadow on the history of our neighbors from across the pond.

Now, orphans were also housed in workhouses, which leads us to the story of a woman who grew up in an English workhouse named Jane. In her book, Shadows of the Workhouse, Jennifer Worth tells Jane's story. Worth describes Jane as an extremely anxious person as an adult. She was scared pretty much of everything and anything. Worth writes that she lived in a state of sheer terror that she would do something wrong.

But according to Worth, Jane wasn't always this way.

Jane's mother was a servant to a member of parliament, a high-class gentleman. And when his wife found out that he had an affair with this young servant girl that caused her to be pregnant, well, they sent the pregnant servant girl and her soon-to-be-born baby to live in the workhouse.

Young Jane and her mom were separated soon after birth, and Jane would never know her mother. So the workhouse was all Jane knew in her early years. But there was something remarkable that distinguished Jane from the other girls in the workhouse. Jane was lively. She was joyful. While the other girls were typically saddened by the predicament that they were in. Her joy, Worth tells us, was rooted in the fact that she held out hope that she would one day see her daddy.

That he would come and rescue her from the workhouse. She would constantly fantasize about being whisked away by her father.

And that she would daydream about what it would feel like to have her father look at her with loving care in his eyes. To Jane, her father could do no wrong.

She even dreamt what his house would look like. And how one day her father would lead her by the hand to see the many rooms in this large mansion that she thought of in her mind.

She believed that one day her father would come to take her home. Jane's dream, however, was only a dream.

Her father would never come to rescue her. Instead, she suffered violent physical abuse at the hands of the master of the workhouse. All the joy and happiness the young seven-year-old Jane had was ripped away from her.

It was ripped away when she realized that daddy was never coming to take her home.

Jennifer Worth's story of Jane is one of countless stories of people who've been forsaken in this world. Who've been forsaken by their loved ones. People who daydream about what it could mean to be loved by those who love them. Or who should love them. People who've experienced the bitter agony of abandonment.

It's possible there's someone here tonight who has a similar story to tell. Maybe you've been abandoned by a mother.

Maybe you've been abandoned by a father like young Jane was. Or perhaps a lover or a friend has cast you to the side. Someone who was supposed to love you, but has instead turned their back on you.

Maybe, like Jane, you've experienced the feeling of when a dream is torn from your grip and it's crushed to pieces.

Or maybe you've turned your back on someone like Jane's father, leaving them to deal with heartache and with having to pick up the pieces of a broken life and shattered dreams.

All of us, to some degree, have tasted the bitterness of abandonment. The agony of broken relationships.

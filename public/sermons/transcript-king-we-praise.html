The King We Praise

Rich Richardson · Jul 20, 2025

Praise The Lord

Souls were also saved at youth camp. I had the privilege of speaking with an eighth grader from Tucson, a young man, who told me that God had changed his life as a result of one of the sermons, and he no longer wants to disobey his parents. I prayed with a young lady from another church whose father battles addiction, and as a result, he has spiritually abandoned his family. He's turned his back on them, essentially, and as the oldest sibling in her family, she's been carrying a lot of weight.

She believed that the Lord used that time at camp to minister directly to her heart. She felt like the Lord removed the burden that she's been carrying, and she felt that the Lord gave her peace. I prayed with another young lady who was wrestling with whether or not she is really a Christian, and I was able to encourage her and help her see that if she belongs to Christ, there is no one that can snatch her from the Father's hand. By the end of the worship night, I saw her boldly approaching God's throne, lifting her hands with a huge smile on her face.

I strongly believe the Lord allowed our students and leaders at camp to taste and see that the Lord is good, and as a result, our students, they walked away with an awe for God that might not have been there previously. Now, as the pastor who oversees our youth ministry, it was especially sweet to spend a lot of uninterrupted time with our students and leaders, and being up there in the pines, it stirred my soul and put wind in my sails to do this for another year, two years, three years, ten years, however long the Lord calls me to this task. But as I was reflecting upon our time up north, I started to think about many of you who have been serving the Lord for years, decades even.

Now, here at Center Church, we have a vibrant seniors ministry. Many of you have been pressing into the Lord, and it's sweet to hear reports from Steve, who just raves about our seniors ministry.

A lot of you have wisdom to teach our young people that we need to tap into. But there are others, others of you here who've served the Lord for many years, and you may be feeling like you've lost your youthful passion. Maybe you feel like you've lost zeal for the Lord. Maybe over time, your awe for God has dissipated. Maybe as life has continued on, and you've fought the good fight, you've grown weary from battling doubts. Weary from battling and waging war against doubts that confront your soul daily. Maybe you've grown weary from anxiety you experience over the medical condition you're battling.

Maybe you've grown weary over reports from doctors. Now, I believe just like the Lord stirred up the hearts and the faith of our young people up north and instilled within them an awe for God, I think the Lord wants to do that in you this morning. I think there are some of you who need your heart stirred up for the Lord, for his purposes. I believe he wants to minister to your heart and put wind in your heart sails for the next decade, two decades, three decades, however long the Lord has for you to walk with him. I pray that this message does that just for you. Maybe you've grown cold in your affections for God. It's an easy thing to do. Maybe you're struggling with spiritual complacency.

This passage from Psalm 145, it has the power to produce in us awe-inspiring praise.

I want to let Paul Tripp introduce this psalm to us, because he has some very helpful words about who we are and about how this psalm can serve us. Tripp writes, there's something that you and I tend to do. We tend to make life all about us. We tend to shrink our lives down to the small confines of our wants, our needs, and our feelings. We tend to put ourselves in the center and make life all about us. It's a me-istic way of living. No, that's not a word. I made it up.

Somehow, everything becomes about how it affects us. Everything is about our wants, our goals, our plans, our emotions. And the fact of the matter is, self-focus, self-interest, me-ism, never produces a happy life. It never produces healthy relationships. It never makes you thankful. It never produces love for God and worship of God. And so, Psalm 145 sits in the psalms to confront our me-ism with the truth that we were created to live for something vastly bigger than ourselves.

Friends, our task this morning is to see just that. To see how we've been created to live for something vastly bigger than ourselves. Because as Tripp points out, what stands in our way of living for something bigger than ourselves is, in fact, our very selves. We get it in the way. Because we want things our way. That's why the Burger King slogan resonates with so many of us. Have it your way. We esteem ourselves higher than we ought to, and we steal the glory that God deserves because we think we deserve that glory for ourselves. Or in Tripp's words, we're glory thieves.

What Psalm 145 shows us is that living the good life requires looking away from ourselves.

Looking away from our circumstances. Looking away from our suffering, from our situations, and looking upward to God.

This psalm is the last psalm of David's in the psalter. It's a call to worship. It's a hymn of praise. And it was carefully crafted by King David to express wholehearted worship.

But this psalm is not relegated to just be an Old Testament anthem.

It finds its true voice in Christ, who alone is our great worship leader.

Jesus is our King, who reveals God's greatness and his goodness to us. And it's only in Jesus, friends, that we can join David in declaring along in the first verse with him, I will extol you, my God and my King. Here's what I want to propose to us. Here's our big idea for this morning. Jesus calls us to praise God daily, proclaim his greatness to others, and rest in his goodness forever. So I'm going to pray. Things are going to be a little different this morning. I'm going to read the psalm within each point. So we'll make it through the first nine verses as we traverse the first three points. But let me pray.

Lord God, we come to you this morning grateful. Grateful that you are our God and that in Jesus we have our great King. Father, I pray that you would remove distractions. Help me as I open your word, as I read from it, as I preach this morning, to look away from myself and look to you. Lord, I pray that you'd minister to our hearts this morning the truth of the gospel. Plant it deep within us and increase our awe for God. In your son's precious name we pray. Amen. Our first point is praise the greatness of God in Christ. The word reads, starting at verse one of Psalm 145, "I will extol you my God and King and bless your name forever and ever.

Every day I will bless you and praise your name forever and ever. Great is the Lord and great to be praised. His greatness is unsearchable." David here opens with this personal vow of praise. "I will extol you. Every day I will bless you." What he's getting at is he's not just wanting to praise God on the Sabbath day. Not just at church or in the temple. Not just when he feels like it. Praise didn't depend on if things were going well for David. Praise for him didn't depend on if everything was going according to plan. His praise of God was a lifestyle.

It was an everyday sort of habit. Regardless of his circumstances, David daily commits to extolling God. Extolling God, which simply means esteeming God or looking upon God with admiration. Why is David compelled to do this every day? Why has this become his daily habit? Well, notice the reason. It says, "Because great is the Lord." His greatness is unsearchable. For David, this is the ground upon which he stands. This is his grounds for his daily praise habit. Now that word unsearchable means that you'll never get to the bottom of it.

No matter how long you live. No matter how deeply you study God's word. No matter how much you know the original languages. No matter how much you worship God, how many instruments you play. We can never exhaust God's greatness. This was the sort of praise David committed himself to. And this is exactly the sort of praise we must be after as well. There is this report that came out recently released by a non-profit named the Ocean Discovery League that estimates that oceanographers and marine biologists have only directly observed somewhere between 0.0006% and 0.001% of the ocean's deep sea floor. This means that since deep sea exploration really started to take off in the 50s, we've barely even dipped our toes into the waters of deep sea research. 99.99% of the ocean floor has yet to be observed by human eyes.

Friends, when it comes to things that are literally unsearchable, think of the ocean floor. So when David talks about the greatness of God being unsearchable, it simply means that no matter how hard we try to study God and know him, we can only ever barely scratch the surface. This past year in youth ministry, we did a series on about God's attributes. And one of the attributes we covered, this is a mouthful, was God's incomprehensibility. It's a lot of syllables. No matter how much we think we have God figured out, we can never truly figure God out. Now the goal here was to help the students see that because God created us, we're creatures who are dependent on him. We're limited. We're finite. And this limits how much knowledge we can acquire of the almighty.

And this is good. This is actually good news. You see, we live in the information age. And we have access to things like ChatGPT and Google, and we think we can figure everything out. But ChatGPT and Google, they can't perform open-heart surgery. We need to go to experts who've acquired a vast array of skills and knowledge to be able to do these sorts of surgical procedures. Someone who knows way more than you or I do about the human heart. Now, I don't know about you, but there's a certain peace I feel when I'm sitting in the dentist chair, and he's about to put a big needle in my mouth, knowing that this guy, well, he better know what he's doing. It should be freeing for us as Christians to know that some things are reserved for the Lord alone. Some things are reserved for the Lord alone.

Some things like, why did God take my baby? Some things like, why did God take grandma so soon? Some things are reserved for the Lord alone. He withholds certain things from us because he knows our frame. He knows that we're weak and that we would not be able to bear up under the pressure of too much knowledge. But the good news is, he has revealed what's essential for us to know. While his greatness is unsearchable, he has revealed his greatness to us. I know that sounds paradoxical, but let me explain. How has the Lord shown us his greatness? By showing us Jesus.

Christ himself is the ultimate revelation of God's greatness. Hebrews 1:3 tells us that Jesus is the radiance of the glory of God and the exact imprint of his nature. Do you want to see the greatness of God? Look at Jesus. Go to the Gospels. Read about who Jesus is. Read about what he's done for us. That's how we see the greatness of God. We need to only look at his perfect life. His merciful heart displayed to us through his death and his victorious resurrection.

We don't need to look any further than to Jesus to see the greatness of God. But all too often, we're blinded by so many other things. So many things blind us. We can't look to Jesus because a million other things obstruct our view of him. A million other things try to rob us of our awe of God. So I want to ask the question, what is it that's robbed your awe of God lately? Friends, this is important. Most of the time, sin robs us of our awe of God. We give ourselves over to sinful pleasures we know are dishonoring to God. We cave at the very first sight of temptation because we're not beholding the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ deeply enough.

Sin robs us of our awe of God because we don't want to put to death what is earthly in us. Sexual immorality, impurity, passions, evil desires, and covetousness. Because we trick ourselves into thinking we deserve those creature comforts for ourselves because of all of the hard work we've done. And then there are other things that rob us of our awe of God. Discouragement robs us of our awe of God. Weariness robs us of our awe of God. Discontentment robs us of our awe of the Lord and the things that He's done. But there is one more thing that robs us. And it's one we're all prone to give into. There's not a one of us here that are excluded from this.

And that's forgetfulness. Forgetfulness robs us of our awe of God. Simply stated, we forget what God has done for us in Christ. We forget the sinful lifestyle that we once indulged in. That lifestyle that Jesus delivered us from by His death and resurrection. We forget that we were at one time enemies of the cross. Rebels who deserved punishment, but who have instead received grace and mercy. That's what we forget. We forget the sweetness of the gospel because we delight ourselves in the bitterness of sin. When we're tempted to forget what God has done for us in Jesus.

We can go to places like Psalm 145. We can combat our sin with praise and say, Lord, I will extol You because You are my God and my King. That sin is not my God. Lord, You alone are God. We can obliterate our despair and discouragement with worship by blessing the name of the Lord and committing to do so daily. We can destroy our forgetfulness by proclaiming that God is great. His greatness is unsearchable. And the good news here is we don't need to muster up the strength to do this on our own. We don't need to try to figure this out for ourselves. Because we're led in this praise by none other than our King, Jesus Christ.

In Hebrews 2.2, quoting Psalm 22, Jesus says, I will proclaim Your name to my brothers and sisters. In the midst of the congregation, I, Jesus says, will sing Your praise. Christ is not only the object of our worship. Friends, this is a monumental truth.

He is the perfect worshiper. And He is the perfect worship leader. Jesus always leads us in triumphal procession. And through us spreads the fragrance of the knowledge of Him everywhere, 2 Corinthians says. So if you've been battling discouragement, if despair has been heavy on your mind and soul, you only need to look to Jesus, your worship leader, who leads us in our praise of God. We can look to Him. And we can praise God's greatness. Because in Him, there's grace to be found. So we're called to praise the greatness of God in Christ, and we're called to proclaim His greatness to others. That's our second point.

Proclaim the greatness of God to others. Verses 4-7 read, One generation shall commend your works to another, and shall declare your mighty acts. On the glorious splendor of your majesty, and on your wondrous works, I will meditate. They shall speak of the might of your awesome deeds, and I will declare your greatness. They shall pour forth the fame of your abundant goodness, and shall sing aloud of your righteousness. Praise doesn't stop with us on a Sunday morning. Praise is meant to be passed on. It's like a baton in a relay race. It's intended to be passed along to the next generation. What David does here is he paints a picture of people who are constantly telling each other about the greatness of God.

Generation to generation, friend to friend, saint to saint, older person to younger person, mom of young children to single college students, young professional to high school student, empty nester to brand new parents. What's happening is they're declaring, they're meditating upon, they're speaking, they're proclaiming, and they're singing the works of God to one another. There's a lateral movement that's taking place. There's a culture of worship being established.

There's a culture of discipleship being created where everyone participates. Not just the pastors. Not just the worship leaders. Not just those serving in children's ministry or at the coffee shop or at the front greeting you. All of us. All of God's people participate in this culture of worship and discipleship. And how do we participate? We do so by commending God's works to one another. By speaking of what the Lord has done. By talking about it.

My heart as the pastor who oversees our youth and children's ministry is not, I repeat, is not that these ministries would be the place where kids hear the gospel for the first time. Now, there may be the unique and exceptional circumstance when a non-believing family checks a kid into our children's ministry. Or they drop them off for VBS. We praise God for those opportunities. But those are not the norm. Instead, your kids should be hearing the gospel regularly in your home. The primary responsibility for discipleship of our youth and children rests not with me. Not with any of the other pastors. Not with the culture. Not on TV.

Not at school. It rests with you. The parents of our youth and children. You see, youth and children's ministry, they exist to serve as auxiliary ministries. They're meant to support what you're called to do as a parent. Biblically, we base this on Deuteronomy 6, which reads, Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one. You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your might. And these words that I command you today shall be on your heart. What is it that we're supposed to teach our kids diligently?

That the Lord is our God. That he is one. That we should love him with our entire hearts and our minds and souls. And how often should we as parents be doing this? All the time. Quite literally, we're told to always do this.

Notice, he says, we should be doing this diligently when we're sitting around the house, when we're on the way to the store. At bedtime, in the morning. If you have a child, the reason the Lord has blessed you with that little one is to disciple him or her. Our goal as parents is to train our children up in the way they should go. In the way that leads to life and not death. It's not to live vicariously through them and be more interested in their academic achievements or their athletic prowess. It isn't to train them to be a better person. A moral, outstanding, morally upright citizen. Yes, we should instill solid morals and values in our kids, but this is not the end goal of parenting. The end goal of our parenting is to see our children grasped what King David grasped and that is the greatness of God to savor the sweetness of the gospel.

There will be bumps along the way because this is not formulaic. It's not promised that only if we do these things, the Lord will save our kids. I know this because some of you have taken this mandate from the Lord very seriously and you've had kids stray. Some of you have diligently taught your children who Jesus is. You've prayed with them. You've pleaded with them to serve the Lord. You've been faithful. The Lord has to be the one to save our children. The Spirit of God needs to be the one to give them new life. All we can do is our part, which is to train them up in the way they should go.

Listen, this was the culmination of gospel labor that you as parents have been working diligently at for years. Nothing is more glorious than that truth. And so when we share the gospel, we're participating and the proclamation that David envisions for us. We're telling the next generation about God's mighty acts in Christ. Discipleship, evangelism, parenting, all these things are all about declaring God's greatness.

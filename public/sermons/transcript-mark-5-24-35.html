One of my heroes in the area of prayer is a man called George Mueller. George Mueller was an Englishman. He lived in the 1800s in Bristol, England. And there's a great autobiography written by Roger Steer that kind of encompasses his life and stories about him. And let me share one of the stories that's in this biography.

One morning, George Mueller was beginning his day as he normally did. He was a man who had a heart to care for orphans. So he built and oversaw large orphanages. This particular morning was like every other morning, except as he prepared to come to breakfast. He received word from his staff that there was no bread in the pantry or milk in the kitchen and not a penny in the account. In other words, there was no food to serve the 300 children coming down for breakfast and no way to go out and buy food for them. We are told how George Mueller responded to this news. He instructed the children to sit down at their tables and to pray with him. George Mueller is reported to have prayed like this. Dear Father, we thank you for what thou are going to give us to eat.

It wasn't a long prayer, particularly a desperate sounding prayer, but it was a prayer of faith. Just moments later, there was a knock at the door and there was a baker outside who said, Mr. Mueller, I couldn't sleep last night. I felt God telling me to bake bread for your children. I got up early and I made enough for everyone. As they were bringing in the bread, there was another knock. This time it was a milkman. He came in and told everyone that his cart had just broken down right in front of the orphanage. And the milk would spoil before he could repair his cart. So the milkman asked, can you use this milk?

That morning, every child had their breakfast. An impossible situation resolved through prayer.

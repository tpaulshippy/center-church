The Only Dichotomy That Matters

Rich Richardson · Jun 8, 2025

Psalm 119:113-120

Aging with Him in His Word. Over the last couple of summers, we've been in a book of the Bible that's written for that very purpose. It's written to increase our love for God and to develop in us a deeper love for His Word. Now the great reformer Martin Luther once commented that Psalm 119 invites and exhorts us to hear and diligently treasure up the Word of God. Charles Bridges, an Anglican preacher and theologian who ministered in the 1800s, once said, Psalm 119 contains the anatomy of experimental religion. The distinguishing characteristic of the family of God. Now Bridges and others who use this term experimental religion, it's used in reference to how we live out what we believe. That's what he means by experimental religion. And so our task this morning, it's really rather simple. I want us to see how this psalm, Psalm 119, verses 113 through 120, contain the anatomy of experimental religion.

Or in other words, I want us to see how Psalm 119 can show us how to live.

Now the reason we need help in this is because everywhere we go, choices abound. You go to the grocery store and you are faced with a plethora of choices.

Clothing stores are the same. Recently I was at a store trying to buy some pants and I was paralyzed with fear over making a decision because there are so many different options. You have boot cut, athletic taper, skinny, relaxed straight, relaxed taper, slim straight, slim tapered, wide leg, and I'm sure there are many, many more. Ladies, you probably feel that too.

Another thing is you can't even talk on your phone or around your phone or else it's going to hear you. And then it's going to inundate you with marketing and ads for products you didn't even know that you needed.

Have you thought about where you're going to eat lunch after the service? There are many places to eat around this vicinity. So you better start thinking about it. Actually, don't do that. Pay attention because we're going somewhere. Look, my point is, as people who live in a world that is full of choices, we all need help with the most important choice. There's only one choice that matters. It's living for God or you can choose to live for yourself. Living for God or living for the world.

Now, what our passage does is it shows us that in God's economy, there are only two choices for how one can live. You either take the path of the righteous or you're on the path of the wicked. Now, for us to see this, we're going to look at the text to answer three important questions for us.

First, who are the wicked? Second, who are the righteous? And third, who are we? It's an existential question, isn't it? So what we're going to do now is we're going to read Psalm 119 beginning in verse 113. And our goal together is to see how God is a refuge for the righteous and how he offers hope even to the wicked.

This is God's word addressed to us from Psalm 119.

It's a fascinating time to be alive, and it's pretty scary. Another fascinating phenomenon in our cultural moment has been the popularity of villain origin stories.

Now, these backstories of characters who were once considered bad guys have continued to captivate the hearts and minds of a generation.

One such movie is Maleficent. It's a Disney movie that draws audiences in with a story of love, betrayal, and then she loses her fairy wings. What the movie does is it reveals that things are more nuanced than they seem, and that Maleficent wasn't always, well, Maleficent. She wasn't always evil.

And how could we miss the most recent iteration of a villain's backstory? One that's taken the world by storm, and one that will have a sequel coming out real soon, Wicked. A screen adaptation from the musical Wicked tells the story of Elphaba before she became the Wicked Witch of the West. What was once considered wicked and vile has been recast in new light. Moral norms that were once clearly delineated and even appropriated broadly by society, such as the identification of what is wicked, are now seen as obstacles to social progress and one's own personal happiness.

What was once wicked and despised is now celebrated and revered?

We cannot look to culture or society for help to determine what is right and wrong because culture and society don't even know the difference between what is right and what is wrong.

So what the psalmist does is he introduces to us a group of people that he calls double-minded in verse 113. What does it mean to be double-minded? It means someone who goes back and forth between two opinions. It refers to someone who claims loyalty to God yet who lives a life that is completely disloyal to God.

Matthew 6.24 helps us to tease out this idea of double-mindedness when Jesus says, No one can serve two masters.

For either he will hate the one and love the other or he will be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and money. You cannot claim to live for God when you don't live for God. Seems simple. Really is. But that's who the psalmist is calling out. He has an intense hatred for those who are double-minded. People who claim loyalty to God but deep down they know they aren't. They disguise themselves as one of us looking the part to receive the benefits of being one of us. Kind of like a mimic octopus.

A mimic octopus can disguise itself as coral or as shrimp as a crab or even a sea snake and it does this to evade its predators. a mimic octopus uses its ability to disguise itself to its own advantage.

Then in verse 115 the psalmist refers to evildoers. These are people who deviously plan to tempt believers into sin.

That's why the psalmist prays depart from me you evildoers.

A godly man or woman wants no part in the wickedness and evil that's done by non-believers. That's the point here. The wicked are characterized by a desire to see Christians caving into temptation and wandering away from the Lord.

Yes, we're called to evangelize and pursue the lost. Yes, we are called to welcome people into our homes and live lives that are open and share the gospel with them.

But when this comes at the expense of our own holiness and pursuit of the Lord there's a problem.

If you're single there's no such thing as flirt to convert.

We should all be wise and walk circumspectly with the way that we approach non-believers. But the problem is is when non-believers begin to evangelize you.

They begin to tempt you by questioning your faith. They begin to tempt you by putting obstacles to your personal growth and holiness in your path.

That's what's inside the heart of those who the psalmist refers to as the evildoer.

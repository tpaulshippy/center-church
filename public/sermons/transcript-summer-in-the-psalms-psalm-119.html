Summer in the Psalms- Psalm 119

Rich Richardson · Jul 28, 2024 · 00:26:38

Trust Him Because There is no Word like our God's Word

And the rest of us can open our Bibles to Psalm 119. We'll be in verse 89 through 96.

No matter where you go or who you speak with, the quiet hum of distrust exists. This distrust has been part of human history, but it gained significant awareness somewhere around 1949 in at least the West, when George Orwell articulated it in his little book, 1984.

Orwell forecasted a future where truth would be constructed to suit people's needs rather than discerned and shared. The entire premise of the Orwellian world was rooted in the slogan, war is peace, freedom is slavery, ignorance is strength. Each contradictory statement reveals and hides the purpose of those in leadership. It shows their purpose in manipulating for their own gain, often at the expense of others, and attempts to hide the danger and wickedness of their ways by eliminating objective reality altogether. Orwell peeled back history for us to see that there was no shortage of reasons to distrust what we are told and shown. There is always a reason for suspicion, and if we're not suspicious, we just might end up a patsy under the thumb of a tyrant. Today, we see the impact of such distrust in our daily lives, at least on our news feeds. Of course, history would also prove that the suspicion, when unfounded, had the potential to wreak as much devastation as its counterpart.

During the era of McCarthyism, many were accused and, at minimum, tarnished in public over nothing more than a dim flame of suspicion.

As dangerous as the manipulation of truth is, so also is the devastation of unfounded allegations in the name of avoiding it. Then there's us. Some 80 years after Orwell's message and McCarthy's hunt, how are we supposed to go about our world? Should we spin our wheels trying to keep up with fact-checking and validating everything we're told or read? Or should we remain heads on a swivel, always on the lookout for danger, or maybe bury our heads in the sand hoping for the best? Should we distrust everyone and just move to the mountains and can vegetables for the rest of our life? Who can we trust? Who can we rely on?

Amid this pervasive distrust, there is a source of truth that remains unshaken. This morning we will turn our attention to Psalm 119 verses 89 through 96 to understand why we can place our trust, all of our trust, in what God says, which is starkly contrasting the unreliable words of men. In moments of doubt and uncertainty, I want us to turn our focus to the word of the Lord. What does the creator, sustainer, deliverer, and ruler of all want us to see and hear in these times of widespread distrust? He wants us to understand, to understand, to know, to hold tightly that we can trust Him. For there is no word like our God's word. Now let's read from Psalms 119 verses 89 through 96. For ever, O Lord, Your word is firmly fixed in the heavens. Your faithfulness endures to all generations. You have established the earth and it stands fast.

By your appointment, they stand this day. For all things are your servants. If your law had not been my delight, I would have perished in my affliction. I will never forget your precepts. For by them, you have given me life. I am yours. Save me. For I have sought your precepts. The wicked lie in wait to destroy me. But I consider your testimonies.

I have given, I have seen a limit to all perfection. But your commandment is exceedingly broad.

It is not lost on me that I would stand here reminding us of all the many reasons that we struggle to trust and yet tell you to trust Him. When the rubber meets the road, we are all faced with the same questions and fears that cause us to question anyone who solicits our trust. Those fears that cause us to doubt, that cause us to pull back and tremble are often not rooted in make-believe. They are very often rooted in a lifetime, an experience of hurt, scandal, lies, betrayal. Our most significant difficulty in trusting this morning is all of the examples of hurt from misplacing that trust in the past. Maybe even this morning, you are experiencing these for yourself. For us to consider the cry of God's word. For us to consider the cry of this passage and the call of Scripture, we must understand why this is different.

It's not the same. Why is God's word unlike all the others? The truth is that God's word is unlike all others first and foremost because there is no one like our God. To see this, we need to look at the text closely and maybe from different angles, not as if it is hidden, but because it is placed with precision by the divine author. Psalm 119 and all psalms are ancient Semitic poetry, where we measure poetry in our culture with phonetical rhyme and rhythm. The psalms are measured by their conceptual patterns and direction. The patterns of this psalm are a road map to a place of clarity for us, where wisdom and insight lie. Most often as we consume the text, we start to see these little arrows through the words that lead our hearts in a direction and finally arriving to see with the author the grandeur of God.

Then sometimes, as in our text, there are these massive signs that you can't ignore, that are meant to be seen from a mile away. That is always in view when we follow the psalmist. Each step taking us through the psalm, all the while knowing where it's going, just not necessarily how we're going to get there. And our passage starts this way. He opens by making it abundantly clear that there is no one like our God. In English we read, forever, O Lord, Your word is firmly fixed in the heavens. But when recited in the Hebrew, the reader is forced to pause, to stop, and break up these statements, sounding something like, The Lord is forever. Your word is fixed in the heavens. Now, this may not seem like a big deal, unless you're a nerd like me or something like that. But it is a massive blaring sign for us to see and make our way through the rest of the text.

First and foremost, we must know that He is forever. There is no beginning and there is no end. Now, before you worry about your translation, I want you to see how logically we can catch this from the same wording. Forever, O Lord, Your word is firmly fixed in the heavens, screams what is carried by the second line, that there is no division between who He is and what He said. He is unlike any other in that His speaking is not contrary at all in any way to who He is. Who He is is forever.

What He says is what He does. It cannot and will not and will and has never change. And why does this matter?

It matters because we don't know anybody like this. It matters because He's unique. God is not man that He should lie or a son of man that He should change His mind. He has said and will He not do it? Or has He spoken and will He not fulfill it? As Numbers tells us. To trust Him, to trust His word, we must see first that He is not like any we have seen or known before. Unlike those we come across in life, there is no chasm, no separation between what He says and what He does. We must know that He was not spun up or He would surely spin out. We must know that He did not come from somewhere or He would certainly go away.

We must know that He does not change in any way or we would expect Him to see change in every way. We must understand that He must understand that He is not dependent on anything or we would surely find no confidence depending on Him. And we must know that He is forever, as it says, or we would wonder surely what comes next. The truth is that the biggest advocate for distrust is the unpredictability of change. And with the Lord our God, there is no shadow or variation due to change. So we can trust Him because there is no word like our God's word. This trust hangs on who He is. What He has proven of Himself. What He has proven so much to us in our own lives. The psalmist reminds us that His faithfulness endures to all generations or, as the text says, from generation to generation.

The text here indicates that it is His faithfulness of doing what is right or true. The breath of this acting in a good and right way is not confined to a single generation.

Recipes for amazing pasta can be confined to a generation. But the faithfulness of God, it goes forth and it covers generation after generation. The breath of His acting in a good and right way is not confined, as we said, but exceeds from one to another. It is not as if His faithfulness can be summed up in one generation, but it requires all of history for us to begin to see the capacity that God has for faithfulness. It is the difference between dropping the stone on a ground and watching an avalanche take the side of Mount Everest to the earth.

He does not simply hit at it. But He overwhelms the families and generations of the earth, one civilization after the other, both with the history of His faithfulness and the up-close-in-your-face demonstration that we all know personally.

Undoubtedly, it is amazing to understand that God has created all things, but it is simply incomplete. It is not enough to say that God has created. For many would say this, but they would go on about their lives, you know, not struggling to believe in God necessarily, but definitely doubting that He would somehow have control of it. After all, look around you. Does not the world scream, something is broken? However, through His Word, the Spirit of God would have us consider how He has not only established the creation, but He ensures it is also sustained. He is different from me, and I suspect some of you, who would start a project half-interested and then leave it there year after year, half-finished, never to be picked up again. His purposes are never abandoned. And what He means to do is complete even before He starts. Therefore, He is not only the means of what He says, but He sees it through.

Think of how unusual this is for us. Indeed, among our work colleagues, our family, and friends, we often wonder who is reliable according to what they say and their best intentions. These are the sort of things that bring up a lack of trust in us to begin with. They plant seeds of skepticism in our hearts, so that when the psalmist makes the point to tell us that the Lord is forever, and that He is established and keeps the earth where it is, we not only know, but we can look back and find thousands of years of good reasons to trust Him. Because there is no word like God's Word.

And it is here as we progress to verse 91 that we start to tremble and we find the limits of our understanding. The psalmist tells us that the judgments of the Lord are fixed and that all things are His servants, meaning essentially that there is nothing, nothing at all, that does not do the bidding or the work of God Himself. No matter our tolerance for complex theological concepts, these statements challenge all of our understanding and intuition. Because anybody who has lived any length of life, who has spent any time around anybody in pain, who has known loss, knows that life is full of difficulty and surprises of the most horrific kind. And all of them know that life is full of this and most of it is unexplainable. So the psalmist wants us, I would think, to pause and to consider the implications of these words, that all things are His servants.

Think about your life.

Think about all those uncontrollables, all those horrific things that you wish weren't a part of it. And hear the psalmist say that all things are His servants. And in our stopping, when we consider the consequences of all that's said before, that God, who is forever, whose words drew out the heavens and the earth from nothing, who designed and constructed all things and intends to their every need, whose faithfulness exceeds the lifetime of men and their family line, is also, somehow, beyond our comprehension, supreme even over tragedy. It is in this place of contemplation where we stand still and hear the whisper of our distrust start to fade into the whirlwind that is Job's words to the Lord.

I know that you can do all things, and that no purpose of yours can be thwarted. Who is this that hides counsel without knowledge? Therefore, I have uttered what I do not understand, things too wonderful for me, which I do not know. Brothers and sisters, how do we know we can trust Him? There is no word like our God's word. He speaks, and tragedy and blessing alike obey. These and everything in between serve His purposes. And how can we know His purposes are good for us? How can we know that His ways are worth our trust? This too is answered by the psalmist.

It is answered in that there is nothing that He gives to His people but life. All He does, promises, and builds is to one end that we would know and have life. From creation to fall, life hung in the balance. From Noah to Abraham, it was the promise and provision of life. From Joseph to Joshua, the people are kept, delivered, covenanted with, and provided for all that they would have life. As the servant of the Lord, Moses, stood before them, he says, I call heaven and earth to witness against you today that I have set before you what? Life and death, blessing and curse. Therefore, choose life. And you and your offspring may live, loving the Lord your God, obeying His voice, and holding fast to Him. For He is your life and the length of days, that you may dwell in the land, and that the Lord swore to your forefathers, to Abraham, Isaac, and to Jacob, to give them.

From judges to the prophets, from the exile to the rebuilding of the temple, life was always laid before the people. When God the Son emptied Himself becoming a man, He faced every temptation endured to the end, never giving an inch to sin or wickedness, becoming a servant like the world had never seen, and walking into a death He did not deserve, and He did it for life.

And He says, thinking of this, this is eternal life. That they, meaning us, know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent. And being raised from the dead, sitting down at the right hand of God, and continually interceding for you, and I, and every believer, He does it so we can have life. Sitting on His throne of righteousness, holding all things together, and seeing every injustice, hearing the cries of His people, He sustains us all, so we can have life. Giving to us the Spirit of God to dwell in us, among us, as the church. He supplies all of our needs and comforts, in all of our afflictions, and sends us out into the dying world. Why? So the world, your neighbors, your grocery clerks, and teachers, your real estate agents, and your baristas, and countless others can have life. Brothers and sisters, when the day of the Lord comes, as it surely will, when the Lord Jesus descends, and we are raised with Him in glory, it will be so that we can have life.

We will hear with our own ears and see with our own eyes the Lord speak and say, Behold, I am making all things new. It is done. I am the Alpha and the Omega, the beginning and the end. To the thirsty, I will give from the springs of the water of life without payment. The one who conquers will have this heritage. I will be His God and He will be my Son. Make no mistake, from start to finish, our triune God, Father, Son, and Spirit has always been about giving life.

So it is unsurprising that the psalmist is reminded of how he comes to know life.

It has been through the Word of the Lord. Through His speaking, His acting, and His realization as we should, we must consider that it would be for us to know to have this life. What it is for us to not trust the Lord and His Word. This brings us to our challenge today. What is it to trust what the Lord tells us? Unlike the rest of the world, what is it to trust Him? Since we know that there is no division between His saying and doing, we can understand that to trust what He says, namely, that He gives us life, that we can trust in the life that He provides us. More specifically, notice that the psalmist identified the unavoidable outcome of not trusting in the Word act of God. It is destruction.

Death in oppression. Death enslaved to sin. This is the same language as those enslaved in Egypt. It is in His remembering and trusting what God has said and done that He is finding life. He is made alive. Trusting what He has said and done is how we all come to life. We place our confidence in His doing and telling that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures, that He was buried, that He was raised on the third day in accordance with the Scriptures and that if you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, that you will be saved. For with the heart, one believes and is justified and with the mouth one confesses and is saved. Where will your confidence go, friend?

No candidate for your trust can measure up or deliver this kind of promise. Will you hear and trust the Word of God? Will you receive life from the One who is like no other? Do not hesitate but believe.

Surely as He has said it, He has said it to you, friend.

It is there delivered to you to have and to trust in yourself. Do not place your hope in a house of cards but trust in the One whom the earth's foundations stand in obedience to. Trust Him who holds your life in His hands and when you believe Him you will find you have been freed from the grip of distrust and free to walk in the newness of life. We find in this newness of life nothing more than His embrace. We find His provision. We find that there is no place in life where He is not our greatest need. That is the reality for the one who trusts in what God says. It is not as if the trouble in life goes away but the promise of life is not avoiding the avoiding of difficulty or adversity. The promise of life is that it will not overcome us.

Living in light of this we would both see the enemy plotting and in seeing their plotting we filter their efforts through the promise of our deliverance. we do not doubt the presence of enemies seen or unseen but we like the psalmist we fix our eyes on Jesus knowing that He has spoken and promised never to leave us or forsake us. We trust in His power and that it is perfect and made perfect in our weakness. Believing that suffering produces endurance and endurance produces character and character produces hope and hope does not put us to shame because God's love has been poured out into our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us. And as the psalmist leads us to see this when we trust God's word we don't stop needing help in life but we absolutely stop living without it. And as we look across the landscape of this world we recall all the promises made and the gimmicks sold. Don't build your hope on these.

We see the limits of what humanity can promise knowing their limitations look instead to the promise of God Almighty and in awe and in confidence for the first time know that you can trust someone you can trust Him because there is no word like our God's word. We can go out into the world that is only ever going to earn our distrust and we can look to the Lord God Almighty Father, Son, and Spirit and have confidence knowing His word is worthy of our trust because there is no one like Him. There is nothing He gives us but life and there is no place in our lives where He is not our greatest need. let's pray. Let's pray.

Holy Spirit we ask in this time of reflection that You would work a miracle in all of our hearts that You would do what no man can do and You would change our hearts and conform them that we would abandon distrust and we would look to You and we would place all of our trust in You. That we would not look to other men
